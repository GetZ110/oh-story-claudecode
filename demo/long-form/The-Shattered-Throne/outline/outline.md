# Master Outline: The Shattered Throne

## Core Trope and Logline

**Core trope:** Underdog rise in a ruined kingdom, powered by a book-keeping magic system. The last page-bearer of the realm's broken account book lights the city's dead lamps one by one, and every lamp he lights costs the Ash Barons a piece of their power.

**Logline:** Corin Vale, a lamplighter's apprentice in a capital ruled by ash and barons, discovers he can see the Ledger of the Realm, an ancient account book that pays in titles for every oath collected. To relight the First Flame and break the hundred-year Ashfall, he must collect the one debt the barons have spent a century refusing to pay: the debt of the throne.

## Emotional Promise

The reader is promised a specific satisfaction: a powerless boy turns a forgotten bookkeeping ritual into a revolution, and every act of kindness he performs is mechanically rewarded while every cruelty the barons commit is mechanically recorded. The book is warm at the center (found family, a street child, a steward who kept the keys) and cold at the edges (ash, debt, the Collector's patience).

## The Ledger (cheat skeleton)

- The Ledger of the Realm is the old kings' account book. It records every oath sworn in the realm, and every uncollected oath becomes a debt.
- Only a Page-Bearer can see it. The last Page-Bearer died with the last king, a hundred years ago.
- When a Page-Bearer collects a debt (fulfills the oath's terms), the Ledger grants a Title. Titles carry small, specific authorities: Keeper of the Lanterns (flame never gutters near him), Voice of the Fallen (hears the dead speak once a night), Shield of the Unburied (ash-things cannot hold him).
- Each Title brands a page onto the bearer's skin. Each Title also carries a Due: a small recurring act of service. Refusing a Due burns a memory.
- The Ledger is enforced by the Collector, Vex, a spirit of the book itself. Vex is patient, exact, and utterly neutral until a debt is priced.

## Book-Level Payoff Rhythm

```
[new title] → [title used in a small kindness] → [barons react] → [price of the barons' debt rises]
    ↓
[the debt count grows] → [the city sees the lights] → [the barons' own oaths start to break]
    ↓
[the siege] → [the relighting] → [the ledger's last entry]
```

Each volume repeats the shape at a larger scale. The escalation is not in Corin's power (titles stay small and domestic) but in the system's reach: the more lamps he lights, the more oaths the Ledger can call in, and the more of the realm's structure is revealed to have been running on debt.

## Main Lines

| Line | Content | Function |

| A (hard pay) | Ledger entries → titles → city lights → barons weakened | Chapter-by-chapter engine; every chapter has a mechanical beat |

## Style Reference

The book's voice is warm, concrete, and mid-paced, with a fondness for small ritual details (lamp oil, tally marks, kitchen tables). See setting/style.md. The prose must stay in the Lower City's register: plain, sensory, and occasionally funny, never epic until the relighting itself.
