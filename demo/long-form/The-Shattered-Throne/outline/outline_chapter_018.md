## Chapter Outline (Chapter 18)

### Chapter 18: The Regent's Ghost

- Core event: In the lull before the barons' last push, the dead regent speaks through the Voice of the Fallen and tells Corin the truth the whole book has been circling: the First Flame was never a fire, it was the king's oath, and the lamp under the chapel is the throne itself.
- Target words: 2200
- Stage position: Rising action / revelation, chapter 18 of 20; volume 1, unit 4.3
- Unit ID/position: Unit 18 (The Regent's Ghost) / revelation unit, pre-climax
- Target emotion: Awe and clarity; the myth resolves into meaning
- Protagonist goal/key choice: Corin wants the flame's truth; his choice is to hear the regent's whole story, including the part that blames his own bloodline
- Chapter positioning: Ordinary advance with the volume's biggest reveal; the calm inside the siege
- Chapter structure formula: Lull + ghost voice + truth told + burden accepted
- Opening hook: The war horn's silence is louder than the war, and in the silence the regent's voice comes up through the ash
- Payoff: The truth re-frames every chapter: the barons did not steal a fire, they buried a promise, and the lamp is the throne
- Forbidden early release: The final entry's text, the siege's end, the barons' fate, Sera's father's choice
- Contract risk: The reveal must answer the mystery, not close it; the regent's blame must cut both ways

#### Content summary (five-part)

- Cause: The lull holds; Corin walks the chapel under the barons' hall alone, the surety cold on his arm / - Development: The regent's voice comes up through the ash: the last regent, keeper of the flame's story, dead a hundred years and never named / - Turn: The truth: the First Flame was the first king's oath to the realm; the throne was the flame, and the flame was the promise; the barons' ancestor buried the lamp to bury the promise / - Climax: The regent names the burden: the lamp is not to be relit as a fire, it is to be sworn as a throne, and the one who relights it becomes the realm's first new king in a hundred years / - Ending: Corin kneels in the chapel and does not touch the lamp, but he asks the question the book has been waiting for: "What does the throne owe the realm?" and the regent's voice goes quiet, because that is the page that was torn out
