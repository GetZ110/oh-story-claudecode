# Worldview: The Cheat System (The Ledger of the Realm)

## What It Is

The Ledger of the Realm is the old kings' account book: every oath sworn in the realm, recorded by hand, with the oath's due date and its collector's name. It is not a spellbook and not a weapon. It is an accounting system with authority, and in a realm that runs on debt, accounting is the only real power.

## Who Can See It

Only a Page-Bearer can see the Ledger. The last Page-Bearer died with the last king. The book chooses its bearer by an unknown test; it found Corin Vale at the moment he lit a lamp he was not paid to light, and it opened its first account in his name.

## How It Works

1. **Entries.** Every oath sworn in the realm is an open line. Uncollected oaths become debts. The book does not care who wrote an entry or whether it was fair; it only cares whether it was sworn.
2. **Collections.** When a Page-Bearer collects a debt (fulfills the oath's terms), the entry is struck through with silver and the book pays the collector.
3. **Titles.** Collections are paid in Titles: small, specific authorities bound to one action. Keeper of the Lanterns (flame does not gutter near the bearer), Voice of the Fallen (one dead voice per night), Shield of the Unburied (ash-things cannot hold the bearer). Titles brand pages onto the bearer's skin.
4. **Dues.** Every Title carries a Due: a recurring act of service the bearer must perform. Missing a Due is settled by paying a memory. The book chooses which memory; the bearer chooses only whether to pay.
5. **The Collector.** Vex enforces the book: the spirit of the Ledger itself, exact, patient, and utterly neutral. It honors every promise the book makes, which is why no one can bargain with it.

## The Rules of Engagement

- Sincerity: an entry must be a real oath, sworn in words the book can record. Half-promises do not open entries.
- Specificity: debts are collected by their terms, never by their spirit. Loopholes are honored; that is the book's one mercy and its one cruelty.
- Compounding: uncollected debts accrue interest across generations. The realm's central catastrophe is a hundred years of compounded oaths.
- Forgery: entries can be forged, and forged entries can be struck. The book records its own corrections, and the barons' greatest fear is the correction of their founding claims.

## Weaknesses and Limits

1. The Ledger cannot create light; it can only keep promises. Every title is a responsibility first and a power second.
2. The bearer is accountable: the book can call any of its own entries and expect collection. Corin's surety in chapter 9 is the book's version of a leash.
3. Forgery cuts both ways: the barons' forged entries were the realm's law for a hundred years, and striking them all at once brought the realm's economy down with them.
4. The book has no mercy: it records Corin's mother's name as a price and takes it without comment.

## Design Notes

The Ledger is the book's thesis made mechanical: the realm's suffering is not caused by villains but by uncollected promises. The cheat's joy is that every act of kindness Corin performs is recorded, priced, and paid; its horror is that so is every cruelty. The system rewards the protagonist for being good, which is the reader's favorite kind of magic, and it charges him for being human, which is the reader's favorite kind of cost.
