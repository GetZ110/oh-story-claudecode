# Worldview: Background of the Shattered Throne

## The Shattering

A hundred years ago the First Flame went out, and the kingdom that had burned around it fell in a single season. The old king died in the Ash Court, the knightly orders dissolved, and five baronial houses divided the realm between them. The capital, Vel-koran, did not fall to an army. It fell to ash.

## The Ashfall

Since the Shattering, ash has fallen over Vel-koran the way rain falls over other cities: constant, grey, and taken for granted. The ash kills what the barons do not bother to kill, taxes what remains, and tastes of salt near the old river. The barons teach that the ash is the realm's judgment on the old king's broken oath. The truth, written in the Ledger's half-charter, is that the ash is the interest on a debt: every unkept oath of the hundred years, compounded.

## The Barons' Rule

Five houses rule from the barons' quarter: the Ash Guard, the tax courts, the fireglass kilns, and the paper mills that print the realm's claims. The barons do not govern so much as invoice. The old court's rituals survive only in the Lower City's lamp room and in the memory of one old steward.

## The Lower City

The lamp district of Vel-koran: three streets of lamps, one gate, and the poorest free people in the realm. It is taxed by the quarter, ignored by the court, and kept alive by its lamplighters. The district's lamps are its only wealth, its only defense, and, after chapter 10, its only flag.

## The Flame's Truth

The First Flame was never a fire. It was the first king's oath to the realm, sworn in the old words, and the throne was its lamp. The barons' ancestor did not extinguish a flame; he buried a promise, under a chapel beneath his own hall, and sealed it with a debt only royal blood could pay. The lamp still burns there, small and silver, waiting for a bearer who can swear the old words.

## Rule Consistency Notes

The world's rules are revealed in the order the protagonist needs them: the ash first (weather), then the debt (the Ledger), then the forgery (chapter 8), then the flame (chapters 12-18). No rule is stated before it can be demonstrated, and the book's greatest reveal re-frames the first chapter rather than contradicting it.
