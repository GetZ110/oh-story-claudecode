# Genre Positioning: The Shattered Throne

## Core Trope

**Underdog rise in a ruined kingdom, powered by a book-keeping magic system.** The book's positioning: progression fantasy where the progression is moral (each title is an act of kindness collected) and the system is bureaucratic (an account book, not a status screen). The throne is a promise, the king is a lamplighter, and the antagonist is a forger who believes his own fiction.

## Reader Promise

The reader is promised three satisfactions in every arc:

1. **The earned power:** titles are collected, never granted; every power-up is a promise kept, which makes the reader feel the system is fair.
2. **The priced kindness:** every good deed is recorded and paid, and every payment has a cost the protagonist chooses; the reader's sense of justice gets mechanical confirmation.
3. **The revealed structure:** the world's misery is a compounding debt, not a villain's whim; understanding the system is the book's version of winning.

## Benchmark Analysis

| Benchmark element | This book's version |
|---|---|
| System interface | An account book in the bearer's hands; no screens, no numbers over heads |
| Power curve | Titles that are small, domestic, and exact (lights, voices, thresholds) |
| Escalation | The system's reach grows, not the protagonist's stats: more lamps, more oaths, more of the realm's structure revealed as debt |
| Antagonist | A bureaucrat-forger who prices people; the system's neutrality is the real antagonist |
| Found family | Mila, Sera, Edric, Rook: each bonded by a debt paid or refused |

## Platform Fit

Royal Road serial, dark-to-warm progression fantasy with a system that rewards kindness. The episodic chapter shape (one title or one cost per 3-4 chapters) fits serial release. The warmth and humor (Mila's chalk, Sera's insults) broaden the readership beyond grimdark; the cost structure (memories, names) keeps the stakes adult.

## Differentiation

Against stat-screen litRPGs, the book offers a system with no numbers over heads and no blue boxes: the ledger's entries are sentences, which keeps the prose warm. Against grimdark, the book offers a system that rewards decency, which keeps the ending earnable. The combination, a moral accountant fantasy, is the book's lane.

## Volume Positioning

Volume 1 (chapters 1-20) sells the system and the city; volume 2 (the Ash Marches) widens the system's scale; volume 3 (the Throne of Debts) settles the realm's account. Each volume's ending is a settlement, not a cliffhanger, with one new entry opened for the next volume.
