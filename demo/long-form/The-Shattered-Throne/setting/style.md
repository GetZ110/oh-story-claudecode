# Style: The Shattered Throne

## Voice Summary

Warm, concrete, and mid-paced. The narration lives in the Lower City's register: plain, sensory, and occasionally funny, never epic until the relighting itself. The book's signature move is to describe the extraordinary through domestic objects: the throne is a lamp, the magic system is an account book, the apocalypse is ash that tastes of salt.

## Sentence Rhythm

Target shape for this book's prose (this is a writing guide, not a statistical report on finished chapters):

| Sentence length | Share | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Short (1-7 words) | 25% | Turns, punches, Mila's declarations |
| Medium (8-18 words) | 60% | The default; narrative workhorse |
| Long (19+ words) | 15% | The relighting, the Vault, moments of awe |

Rules: never run two long sentences in a row; a long sentence is always caught by a short one. Keep paragraphs short in action, longer in the lamp room. Dialogue carries the humor; narration carries the awe.

## Sensory Anchors

- Ash: taste (salt), sound (settling), weight (on shoulders, like a coat).
- Lamp light: smell (oil), texture (warmth at a distance of one hand), color (silver for the Ledger, amber for the district).
- The district: chalk dust, bread, rain on the cart road, the tally of the oil stores.

## POV Discipline

Third person limited through Corin, always. The reader learns the Ledger's rules as Corin learns them, and the book never shows a scene Corin does not witness. Two disciplined exceptions exist: chapter 6's decree arrives through a collector's visit (still witnessed), and chapter 13's informer is revealed through the district's discovery (still in Corin's presence). No head-hopping; the mystery layer works because the reader and Corin carry the same gaps.

## Dialogue Rules

- Characters speak in their register: Corin in arithmetic, Sera in clauses, Edric in court formalities, Mila in chalk facts, Rook in silence.
- Humor comes from character, never from the author: Sera's insults, Mila's sums, Pell's complaints.
- At emotional peaks, let the character say the theme in plain words: "What does the throne owe the realm?"

## Emotion Pacing

One warm domestic scene (kitchen, lamp room, bread) every two chapters. One cost (memory, name, oil) per 3-4 chapters. The book's temperature is maintained by the district: when the city is warm, the prose is warm; when the barons act, the ash does the chilling.

## Prohibited Patterns (house rules)

- No trailer endings: a chapter closes on an action, an image, or a line, never on a summary of what just happened.
- No "couldn't help but"; the book's people choose, or they do not do the thing.
- No negation parades: one "not" per sentence, and prefer positive statements.
- No em-dash clusters; a single dash per paragraph at most, and prefer commas.
- Sentences stay mid-length; the book's awe is built with images, not with long clauses.

## How to Use This File

Before writing a chapter: read the outline's structure formula, then write in the district's register. Keep the arithmetic concrete (oil, lamps, names, chalk), keep the warmth attached to objects, and end on the hook the outline specifies, not on a summary.
