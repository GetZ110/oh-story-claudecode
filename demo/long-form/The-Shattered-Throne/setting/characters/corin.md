# Corin Vale

## Basic Information

- **Identity:** Lamplighter's apprentice of the Lower City, last Page-Bearer of the Ledger of the Realm
- **Core traits:** Patient, exact, quietly kind; keeps accounts in his head the way his master keeps oil
- **Current abilities (titles held):** Keeper of the Lanterns (flame does not gutter near him); Voice of the Fallen (one dead voice per night); Shield of the Unburied (ash-things cannot hold him)
- **Core motivation:** To make the district's lamps stay lit; the ledger's arithmetic is how he loves people
- **Weakness:** He prices everything, including himself; the memory cost of his titles is a hole he keeps filling with duty

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
Seventeen, thin from a childhood of short rations, with lamp-smoke in his clothes and ash in his hair. His left forearm carries the page-brands: two dark pages of the Ledger, and the surety's cold page on his right arm. He moves like someone counting steps.

**Behavior**
- Lights lamps the way other boys throw dice: seriously, with ritual.
- Answers questions with numbers when he is nervous ("Three streets, forty-two lamps, one candle").
- Gives Mila his bread without looking at her, because looking would make it a thing.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
Corin is an accountant by temperament and a keeper by accident. The Ledger found him because he already lived by debts and due dates: the lamp tax, the oil rations, the promise to Pell. His arc is learning that some sums cannot be counted, and that the throne's debt is one of them.

**Goals**
Surface: keep the district's lamps lit, then keep the district alive. Deeper: find the answer to the torn page's question: what does the throne owe the realm?

**Secret**
He does not know whether he wants the throne. He knows he wants the lamps to stay lit, and he suspects the two are the same want.

## Speech Style

- Ledger grammar under stress: "Entry: one debt. Due: tonight. Collector: me."
- Plain warmth: "Eat the bread, Mila. I counted it twice."
- The old words when they matter: he swears the king's oath in the chapel tongue, and the city hears it.
