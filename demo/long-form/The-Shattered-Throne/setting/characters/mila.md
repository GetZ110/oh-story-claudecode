# Mila

## Basic Information

- **Identity:** A street child of the Lower City, Corin's shadow, the realm's unpaid debt made flesh
- **Core traits:** Quick, silent, ferociously loyal; counts everything in chalk because paper is too expensive
- **Current state:** Her father's oath was held and then cleared; she is the district's youngest chalk-accountant
- **Core motivation:** To never owe anyone anything, which is impossible, and to keep Corin alive, which she treats as the same sum
- **Weakness:** She trusts Corin absolutely, and the book has taught her that absolute trust is a debt with interest

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
Ten, small for her age, dressed in a coat of Corin's old clothes with the sleeves rolled six times. A piece of chalk always in her fist or behind her ear. She has the stillness of a cat and the arithmetic of a tax collector.

**Behavior**
- Marks the district's debts in chalk on the walls: names, dates, amounts, hopes.
- Follows Corin at a distance of exactly one alley width.
- Eats bread slowly, in the open, because she has decided no one will take it.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
Mila is the book's moral meter: a child whose father's oath was recorded and compounded, who lives in a district that owes a hundred years of interest it never borrowed. She is not innocent and not wise; she is exact, which is the same thing the book values, and the book notices. Her chalk arithmetic becomes the district's real accounting in chapter 8, and her fear in chapter 9 becomes the chapter's engine.

**Goals**
Surface: keep the chalk, keep Corin, keep the bread. Deeper: to see a ledger with her name in it that says "paid in full".

**Secret**
She was the one who told the district where the informer merchant hid his silver; no one ever asked how she knew, and she has never said.

## Speech Style

- Chalk arithmetic: "Forty names, three streets, one night. The oil does not add up, Corin. I checked twice."
- Absolute declarations: "You do not get to die. I wrote your name on the wall and the wall does not lie."
- Rare, devastating softness: "What happens if you do not find the title?" (chapter 9, asked once, never again)
