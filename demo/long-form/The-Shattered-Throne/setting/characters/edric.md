# Edric Hale

## Basic Information

- **Identity:** Last royal steward of the old court, keeper of the Vault key, the last man in the realm who remembers the flame's story
- **Core traits:** Dignified, exact, spent; carries the court's manners like a coat that no longer fits
- **Current state:** His name was given to the Ledger to open the chapel seal; the book no longer records him
- **Core motivation:** To see the flame's promise kept before he forgets what his name was
- **Weakness:** He has spent everything; what remains of him is purpose, not person

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
Sixty, neat in a patched steward's coat that was once livery. Walks with the upright carriage of a man who served a court that no longer exists. His left hand has a tremor that appears only when he lies, which he has stopped doing.

**Behavior**
- Keeps the old rituals: bows to the flame's direction, refers to the throne in the present tense.
- Counts the district's needs the way he once counted the palace's.
- Holds Corin's arm the way a blind man holds a guide.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
Edric is the realm's memory made flesh, and the memory is killing him slowly. He survived the barons' coup by becoming invisible: a servant among servants, a man without a name's worth. The Ledger gave him back his name's worth in chapter 12, and he spent it the same day on the door that hid the flame. He does not regret it; the book no longer records him, which he calls peace.

**Goals**
Surface: open the chapel, see the flame, close the hundred years. Deeper: to be remembered once, in the old language, by someone who was there.

**Secret**
He is the child of the old steward and a lady of the court, born outside the book's records; that is why the seal could accept his name. He has known since boyhood, and he has never said it aloud until the chapel door.

## Speech Style

- The old court register, unironic: "The realm is in your debt, Page-Bearer. That is not a phrase. It is a sum."
- The tremor appears in his voice when he says "my name": once, in chapter 12, and never again.
- Quiet humor: "I have outlived three kings and one wardrobe. The wardrobe lasted longer."
