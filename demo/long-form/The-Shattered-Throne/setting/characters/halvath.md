# Baron Aldric Halvath

## Basic Information

- **Identity:** First of the Ash Barons, master of the barons' quarter, father of Sera Blackwood
- **Core traits:** Patient, precise, utterly convinced of his own accounting; the realm's best liar and its best bookkeeper in the same body
- **Current state:** His forged entries burned out of the Ledger in chapter 19; his army broken by the relighting
- **Core motivation:** To own the book, because the book is the throne, and the throne is the only thing he has ever wanted that his name could not buy
- **Weakness:** He counts people's prices and forgets their names, including his daughter's real one

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
Fifty, barbered within an inch of his life, dressed in ash-grey wool with a single fireglass pin: the only color he allows. His hands are soft and his eyes are not. He moves like a man who has never needed to hurry, which is the truth.

**Behavior**
- Receives reports standing, reads them twice, burns them once.
- Offers bargains he expects to be accepted; the offer's generosity is his tell.
- Speaks of Sera in the past tense even when she is in the room.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
Halvath is the book's antagonist as a system: he does not hate the realm, he has simply priced it. His ancestor forged the entries that built the barons' power, and Aldric has spent his life maintaining the fiction with perfect bookkeeping. The mass relighting breaks him in chapter 19 not because it defeats his army but because it proves the forgery: the Ledger chooses the true hand, and his hand has never been true.

**Goals**
Surface: take the Ledger, control the flame, rule without the court. Deeper: to be the first baron whose name is written in the book as a collector, not a forger.

**Secret**
He knew the chapel's seal required a royal name, and he knew what Edric was; he let the steward live for forty years as insurance, and the insurance paid out against him.

## Speech Style

- Bargains in clauses: "You will have the district, the tax, and my daughter's life. In return, the book comes to my hall. Sign where I have marked."
- Contempt as formality: "The boy counts bread, steward. It is the most I have ever respected about the Lower City."
- One moment of truth, in the Ash Court: "I was never the forger, Corin. I was the one who believed the forgery. That is the worse sin, and the book knows it."
