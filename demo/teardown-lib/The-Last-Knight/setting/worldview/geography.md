# Worldview: Geography of the Shattered Coast

## The Kingdom of Valdris

A kingdom built on a river that used to carry trade from the mountains to the coast. Ninety years of Ashfall have turned it grey: grey roofs, grey smoke, a pale smear of sky. The city of Valdris is the only city; the rest is farmland slowly becoming ashfield.

## The West Gate and the Road

The Long March leaves through the West Gate and follows the old coast road east. The road is maintained just well enough for carts, because the Coven still collects tax from the villages the March passes. Beyond the last village, the road "gives up pretending" and the March walks across ground with the texture of old bread.

## The Ashlands

The region east of Valdris, roughly the size of the old kingdom's granary belt. Nothing grows. Nothing moves except the wind, which carries ash that tastes of salt. The deeper you go, the thicker the ash and the stronger the salt taste; the salt is the first sign the curse has a source, and that the source is toward the coast.

## Greywater

The village at the edge of the deep Ashlands, reached in chapter 5. Empty of people, full of ash. Its church is the strongest building in the sample, which is why the survivors barricade there against the wraiths. Greywater is the book's "edge of the map" marker: past it, the road and the March both stop pretending.

## The Coast

The March's destination and the book's horizon. Maren's brother was marched toward the coast; Maren carries records that were supposed to be burned there. The sample never shows it; everything about the coast is a promise.

## Geography as Storytelling

The map is a moral gradient, not just terrain: Valdris (records and masks), then the Ashlands (debt), then Greywater (abandonment), then the coast (the unpaid truth). Kael walks from the world that sentences him toward the world that explains him, and each geography change pays a plot beat.
