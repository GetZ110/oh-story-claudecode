# Kael

## Identity

- **Name:** Kael (Kael of the Dawnward Order, name struck)
- **Role:** Protagonist, last knight of a dissolved order
- **Status at analysis end:** Marching east toward the coast, carrying Roran's name; missing his mother's

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
A tall man in his early thirties, gaunt from a winter in the cells. Grey coat, no sigil. He moves the way men move who have learned to conserve strength. The only marker left of his order is the way he stands: feet planted, weight forward, like a man expecting to be charged.

**Behavior**
- Counts things (drips, days, words). Counting is how he keeps his mind off the cold.
- Stops when he decides to stop. The book's signature action.
- Speaks in short declarative sentences; refuses to plead.
- Hands the wardens nothing: no begging, no anger, no information.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
Stubborn by principle, not by temperament. He is not hot-blooded; he is exact. When he says "Put it in the record", he means the record is the only court he recognizes. His code is procedural: he keeps his word because keeping a word is a process, like counting drips.

**Goals**
Surface: survive the Long March, reach the coast. Deeper: keep the names he carries (his own, Roran's, the dead boy's) from being lost in the record. His real goal is archival: he wants things to stay true.

**Secret**
The silver light on his hands. He knows what the Coven does with light-men because Roran told him, and he has not decided whether the light is a gift or a symptom.

## Speech Style

- "It was winter. The granary was full." - facts, no plea.
- "Put it in the record." - imperative, quiet.
- "Cover his face." - an order given to no one in particular, which is how he leads.
- Never jokes, but his gravity reads as dry humor to the reader because the world around him is theatrical.
