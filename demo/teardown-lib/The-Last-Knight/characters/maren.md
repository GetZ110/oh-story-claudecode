# Maren

## Identity

- **Name:** Maren
- **Role:** Core supporting; second viewpoint; archive clerk turned fugitive
- **Status at analysis end:** Walking beside Kael, carrying records that were meant to be burned

## External Presentation

**Appearance**
A young woman in a grey hood that she never removes in daylight. Carries a satchel close to her body at all times, the way a mother carries an infant. Her hands are ink-stained; the stain never quite washes off.

**Behavior**
- Watches before she speaks. She watched the March for three days before joining it.
- Writes everything down, including people who do not know they are being recorded.
- Shares food only after she has decided the person is safe; the bread scene in chapter 3 is her trust threshold.
- Gives information in order of importance, never in order of time.

## Inner Analysis

**Character**
An archivist who stole the archive. Her loyalty is to the record itself, which is why she records Kael before she trusts him: to her, being written down is being taken seriously. She is precise, patient, and privately furious. The fury is what the narration never shows directly; it surfaces only in her handwriting.

**Goals**
Surface: reach the coast with the records. Deeper: find out what happened to her brother, who was marched five years ago. Her real goal is to make the record tell the truth about where the March goes.

**Secret**
She has read the records she stole, and she has not told Kael the full contents. The line she hides is the one that names the kind of man the Coven pays well for.

## Speech Style

- Precise, conditional, occasionally bureaucratic: "That is not in the record" is her catchphrase, borrowed from the clerk who sentenced Kael.
- Gives instructions in numbered steps when the situation is dangerous.
- Never raises her voice; when she is angry she writes.
