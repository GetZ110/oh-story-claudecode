# Storylines: The Last Knight

## Story Framework

**Progression with moral hardening.** The book combines the progression structure (a powerless man acquires a power with visible rules and costs) with a character arc that moves from procedural obedience (keep the record) to principled sacrifice (carry the name, pay the memory). The magic system and the moral system are the same system: oath-silver pays out only for sincere oaths, so the power curve is a morality curve.

## Plotlines

### Main Line: The Long March (chapters 1-5, 100% coverage)

Kael is sentenced to walk into the Ashlands, discovers the curse is a debt of unkept oaths, gains oath-magic, and walks east carrying a dead man's name. This line carries every chapter's spine: the March is always moving, and every scene either advances it or explains what it costs.

Tag: progression / journey / discovery

### Sub-line A: The Record (chapters 1-5)

Maren's hidden ledger, the clerk's record, Roran's name, the burned-archive records: the book's second engine is the act of writing down. Every character in the book either keeps a record, is recorded, or fights the record. This line provides the mystery layer (what do the records say?) and the structural humor (the warden's compliment as an archive entry).

Tag: mystery / bureaucracy / parallel POV

### Sub-line B: The Price (chapters 3-5)

The magic system's cost line. Oath-magic pays in silver and collects in memories. This line escalates every chapter from the first oath (unnamed price) to the specific loss (mother's name). It is the book's emotional spine: each oath makes the next one harder to justify and easier to admire.

Tag: cost escalation / emotional stakes
