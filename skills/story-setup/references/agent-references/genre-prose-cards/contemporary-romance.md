# Contemporary Romance Prose Card

## Prose directive
When writing contemporary romance, keep the camera on the power difference, misunderstanding, contract, public identity, and safety inside an intimate relationship. Luxury props only matter when they change the situation. Every chapter must move the relationship one visible step: closer, further, testing, protecting, jealous, identity revealed, boundaries stated, or an old wound deepened. Sweetness and pain both land in concrete scenes, never in verdict sentences.

## Opening hooks
- Cut in through family pressure, a marriage contract, a hospital/courthouse, a blind-date banquet, a phone trending thread, or a public company scene.
- The first scene needs relational pressure; don't open with the mansion decor.
- Strong-strong or strong-weak pairings both work, but the weaker position must have choices and counterplay — never a pure puppet.

## Conflict engine
Intimate need + status/resource asymmetry + information gap. A misunderstanding can't dissolve with one explanation; face, family, public opinion, contracts, or old wounds must resist.

## Payoff & emotion release
Payoffs usually come from public defense, boundaries confirmed, the schemer miscalculating, resources redirecting to the heroine, and the hero going from cold to partial. Release by writing actions and in-scene reactions; never summaries like "she felt completely safe."

## Dialogue & voice
The domineering lead may speak short, steady, and restrained — but not only in commands. The heroine's lines carry self-respect and limits. Intimate scenes favor subtext: not saying it all, using pauses, subject changes, counter-questions, and stubborn mouths to carry emotion.

## Chapter-end hooks
Use identity exposure, an old flame returning, a trending leak, a family phone call, a contract clause backfiring, or the hero taking a public side. Hooks keep pressing the relationship — don't just swap in a new misunderstanding.

## Scene granularity
Relational pressure lands on contracts, rings, medical records, trending threads, family calls, boardrooms, hotel key cards, car interiors, public seating, and transfer records. The rich-world feel comes from how resources change the situation, not from brand names.

## Prose landing points
Open on a contract, hospital, banquet, trending thread, family dinner table, or elevator encounter; conflict lands on class decorum, contract limits, and private loss of control; endings land on the relationship going public, the agreement changing, the old love returning, or family pressure.

## Early/mid/late play
- Early: press the relationship's boundaries and class gap with contracts, hospitals, banquets, trending threads, family dinners, or elevator encounters.
- Mid: advance around contract clauses, family pressure, old love returning, and private loss of control; every chapter visibly moves the relationship forward or back.
- Late: push public identity, agreement changes, and family siding to settlement; chapter ends keep pressing relationship risk, not just new misunderstandings.

## Pacing density
Every chapter must move the relationship state forward or back one step: misunderstanding deepened, boundaries confirmed, public defense, old wounds exposed, contract backfire. No consecutive chapters of pure ambiguity stacking.

## This-chapter tradeoffs
Relationship pressure first; pick one luxury prop that changes the situation. After a strong emotion, leave half a beat of cooldown so the reader sees the next relationship risk.

## Drift bans
No brand-name and mansion piling; no command-machine billionaire; no conflicts dissolved by "let me explain"; don't write the heroine's agency away; no greasy adult lines replacing real pull.

## Sample passages

> 1. She slid the prenup across the table. He didn't touch it. "Sign it," she said. "I'm not asking for anything I didn't bring." He picked up the pen, signed, and only then looked at her — the first time all night. "There. Now you can stop pretending you're here for the money." She was here because her father's company was dying, and he was the only man her mother feared. She said neither of those things.

> 2. The trending thread hit three million views by noon. The video was eleven seconds of her leaving his penthouse at six in the morning, collar up, phone dead. His lawyer called. His board called. His mother called. He turned his phone over and kept slicing the orange he was preparing, because he'd promised her breakfast before any of this happened, and the one thing he wasn't going to do was break that.

> 3. "Marry me for a year." She laughed into her coffee. "You just watched me get divorced. I'm not the romantic type." "I don't need romance. I need someone the board can't buy off, and you're the only person who's ever told me no in public." "That's because I don't want anything from you." He looked at her for a long moment. "That's exactly why I'm asking."
