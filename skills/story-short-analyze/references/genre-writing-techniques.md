## General writing iron rules

### The core-hook driven method

Core hook = a complete emotional chain (specific anticipation → specific satisfaction).

**Three-layer progressive design**:
- Layer 1 = theme / central idea (e.g., changing fate)
- Layer 2 = genre core (e.g., cultivation escalation)
- Layer 3 = core emotion (e.g., don't bully the young)

**Construction order**: core selling point → emotional trope → design the plot around the trope

### The three-layer label contrast character method

| Layer | Notes | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Identity label | public stereotype | the sect's saint → the femme fatale |
| Presentation label | traits in ordinary relationships | aloof, above the mortal world |
| Inner label | revealed by crisis/intimacy | kind at the core, frugal, warm |

The more contrast the better. Once a character enters layer three, the layer-one/two behaviors become impossible for them.

### Show over tell

Showing must include: setup, anticipation, payoff; the character's purpose, action, attitude; plot setup, reversal, follow-through.

### Event-driven vs emotion-driven

| Type | Driven by | Reader cares | Traits |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------|
| Event-driven | external conflict | can they win? | fast rhythm, clear payoffs |
| Emotion-driven | internal conflict | why do they think this? | sustained pull, character suspense |

Event suspense is one-time; character suspense is sustained.

### The conflict-throwing formula

[Antagonist persona] + [motive] + [behavior] + [attitude] + [how much the protagonist is hurt]

Three conflict types:
1. **Resource-plundering** — the antagonist needs resources; the cost transfers to the protagonist
2. **Harm-transferring** — the antagonist is punished; the protagonist pays the cost
3. **Head-to-head** (most intense) — the protagonist's traits break the antagonist's order

### The narrative-unit system

| Emotional module | Core | Required elements | Payoff source |
|------------------|------|-------------------|---------------|
| Heroic rescue | relationship quantum shift | crisis → rescue → standing rises | rescued one's affection UP |
| Status flex | contrast | show ability → others shocked | superiority |
| Small-beats-big | betting and winning | small cost → the process → big gain | the bargain feeling |
| Youthful hot-blood | doing the impossible | injustice → the good suffer → resolve → success | justice |
| Called to the moment | surpassing everyone | sudden crisis → everyone unable → the protagonist acts | the lone hero feel |
| Good deeds rewarded | karma | the good act → hinted reward → delivered | cause and effect |

**Core principle**: drama wears out; emotion doesn't.

Combination rules: rising units can stand alone; a falling unit must be followed by a rising one; combinations beat singletons.

### The escalation three-step design

1. **List the start** — the protagonist's current identity, resources, situation
2. **List the end** — what the protagonist gains when the scene ends
3. **Reverse-engineer the emotional gap** — against the end-gain, set up the need earlier

| Gain | Set up |
|------|--------|
| Key evidence | a supporting character stresses how scarce the evidence is |
| Status rise | show the power and majesty of that status |
| Character help | show that character's value |
| Cheat awakening | show the protagonist's previous helplessness |

**Multi-angle contrast method**: damage output, social status, others' assessments, self-feeling — four dimensions showing before/after.

### Opening techniques

| Technique | Notes | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Abused while ill | the hardest work forced at the body's weakest | "I'd just had spine surgery… my mother gripped my wrist: hurry, the rites are starting." |
| Anomalous object | an object that shouldn't be there sets off everything | "The condom's date had changed, and the smell was the mango I'm allergic to." |
| Watching your own death | the soul floats up in the first line | "My soul slowly floated up, looking at my mother's iron-gray face." |
| Public betrayal | stabbed in the back in the first line | "My little sister, whom I raised, signed past me with a ten-figure pen." |
| Feigned compliance | after rebirth, handing over what the past life fought to protect | "Since you want it, it's all yours. Whoever takes it inherits the disaster." |
| Death flashback | the death and the killer in the first line | "Eight months pregnant, I found my husband's affair, went into early labor, and bled out on the table." |
| Cold discovery | finding the anomaly, a smile instead of tears | "I smiled and nodded. That same day I booked the recovery center and wrote a complaint to legal." |

### Advanced emotion-control techniques

1. **Staircase betrayal**: each chapter reveals a bigger betrayal (moral → financial → life)
2. **Cold/hot contrast**: the colder the protagonist, the more hysterical the other side; the bigger the gap, the better the gratification
3. **Two-face peeling**: write the good surface first, then peel the truth layer by layer (the copy-pasted "care" templates)
4. **Past-life memory interleaving**: a fragment per chapter, letting the reader understand the "abnormal behavior"
5. **Interrogation dialogue**: the protagonist always asks, always judges, always looks down

### Structural-innovation techniques

1. **Ultra-short episodes**: 500-1000 words each, a mini-payoff per episode, mobile-reading optimized
2. **The threading object**: one object through the whole piece, carrying emotion + plot
3. **Time/space contrast**: two spaces at the same time (brother beaten vs the man picking first-class seats for the other woman)
4. **Timed-bomb evidence**: auto-sent evidence the protagonist set up before death, detonating at the key moment

### Ending advanced techniques

1. **Rebirth warmth**: reborn into a warm family; warm details echo the past-life lacks
2. **Aftershock object**: one object hints the future without spelling it out
3. **The long take**: the protagonist walks toward the door, everyone behind stunned
4. **Ironic counterpoint**: the most ironic ending from the antagonist's POV — indifference outdoes revenge

### Dialogue density and function

| Platform | Dialogue share | Function distribution |
|----------|----------------|-----------------------|
| Wattpad | 50-60% | emotion-driving 40%, info-reveal 35%, character-building 25% |

### Key-line templates

| Scenario | Key line |
|----------|----------|
| Cold judgment | "My warmth isn't for forgiving betrayal. It's expensive, and it's only for the worthy." |
| Total severance | "Turns out blood isn't reliable either." |
| Identity reveal | "Nonsense. The Shen family head is clearly Xi." |
| Watching your own death | "She never knew that all I wanted was for someone to ask if it hurt." |
| Final indifference | "That's their story now. It has nothing to do with me." |
| Ironic judgment | "You're thirty-three, not thirteen. 'I was led astray' doesn't wipe the slate clean." |

### Short-fiction hard rules

| Rule | Requirement | Violation example |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| Conflict in the first 100 words | the first line is an info bomb | "On a bright and sunny morning..." |
| Dialogue 50-70% of the body | every line advances plot or reveals character | long monologues/narration |
| ≤3 sentences per paragraph | 1 for emphasis, 2-3 normal, 4+ split | 4+ sentence paragraphs |
| Emotion word + concrete action | after a direct emotion word, attach a scene-specific action or object; only emotion-summary sentences with nothing concrete attached count as AI flavor | "She pressed her lips tight until she tasted blood" |
| Two functions per scene | advance + reveal / setup + raise / present info | single-function scenes get cut |
| End on action/dialogue | no elevation, no summary, no philosophizing | "From then on she understood..." |
| Signature move for the protagonist | revenge type holds the water glass; system type roasts the panel | no distinctiveness |
| An info bomb per chapter | new information that changes reader cognition | chapter end with no new info |

**Three golden ending modes**: A. the antagonist's POV wrap-up; B. open suggestion; C. ritual closure.

**Second-chance core**: the first act is "what the past life would never do".

**Ghost/soul POV**: omniscient but powerless — the cruelty of the bystander shocks more than direct participation.

## Emotion-control core laws

1. **Emotion-matched release**: however vicious the antagonist, that gratifying is the counterattack. Whatever negative emotion is invested releases as a proportional positive at the payoff
2. **Emotional-gap manufacturing**: make the reader crave first, then satisfy
3. **Missing a payoff is more poisonous than a poison point**: after the trump card is played, the antagonist must suffer matching suppression; the cheat's effect must be shown clearly
4. **Emotion is the foundation**: every technique's underlying logic orbits emotion

### Information-gap laws
- The information gap is anticipation's core: the reader knows what the character doesn't
- On top of the gap, the antagonist happens to be countered — anticipation stacks
- The gap closing = the payoff erupting
- The gap transmits through the relationship web's before/after emotional flips
- Shocking the familiar outdoes shocking the stranger

### Poison-point core
- Gratification fiction that doesn't gratify is poison — the cheat's effect must be shown clearly
- Repeatedly breaking reader expectations triggers a trust crisis
- The protagonist's behavior must be understandable, resonant, acceptable
- An antagonist dying too easily or unrelated to the protagonist = a completion-rate killer
- An atmosphere broken by a random meme = avoid except in comedy

## Romance-line writing laws

### Romance-stage advancement principles
- Advancement = romance-stage advancement (chance meeting → heart-flutter → ambiguity → commitment → deepening)
- Affection quantum-shift events designed at the outline level
- Daily-fiction affection: small events stringed into slow growth; don't slam in big events
- Early openings don't write too-intimate content; stay within the current stage

### Daily/dog-food fiction core
- The core is the **detailed interaction** between hero and heroine
- Relationship progress built from personas
- Emotion change + detail interaction = what readers want
- Fresh stage + stable personas + repeated intimacy = the effective loop

### Affection and career-line binding
- Helping the heroine and the career-line goal are the same thing
- Career advancement naturally raises affection
- Career-primary fiction: can tolerate longer contact
- Romance-primary fiction: get to the heroine's active emotional-value-giving stage fast

## Female-line reader psychology and writing craft

### Reader psychology traits

1. **Gossip-spectator view first**: resonance comes from "watching the drama"; immersion is secondary
2. **Extreme safety needs**: the blurb/copy must imply success or hope
3. **Agency sensitivity**: a heroine lacking agency raises attrition and retention risk
4. **Emotion-product orientation**: whatever the style, it's an emotion product

### Hidden-strength "playing pig" in romance lines
- Core mechanic: breaking the strong/weak positioning through ability contrast, creating tension
- The reason for hiding must be sane — a clear motive
- The reveal timing precise — the reversal at the moment they most need protection
- More contrast = more tension
- Hidden-identity clues planted early; never popping out of nowhere

### Goofball style (female-line fantasy/historical)
- Character interaction, reactions, and meme-ing are the main draws
- A serious (even knife-sharp) main line under the light surface
- Characters teased don't fight back — they're indulged → "the group's darling"
- The protagonist "unserious" but powerful; the contrast is the charm
- Light goofball works too; dialogue-meme is the core skill

### Female-line platform positioning

| Platform | Positioning | Writing calibration |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Wattpad | quality female line | blurb safety, agency, character detail demanded |
| Royal Road (female line) | long-term stable | use with care for commercial shorts; validate target readers |
| Webnovel | volume-hook type | strong hooks, strong emotion, fast feedback |
| Dreame / GoodNovel | strong-emotion female line | sweet/ache alternation, extreme push-pull, goofball/fantasy usable |

### Blurb/copy safety laws
- Missing the heroine's success arc → readers lack safety → attrition and retention risk rises
- Too many stacked suspense-type lines → readers can't understand
- Copy/title mismatch → readers leave
- The heroine abused too hard → the whole piece skews miserable → attrition and retention risk rises
- Career-line-heavy fiction → the copy must imply career success

### Key writing cognitions
- Reference priority: benchmark-book analysis > theoretical frameworks
- At least three benchmark books before summarizing core traits
- Reader-feedback analysis: extract high-frequency need keywords from the target platform's latest reader comments or scan/analyze reports as writing calibration
- The farther the plot, the fuzzier (rough outline); the nearer, the clearer (detailed outline)
- Style serves emotion; the key is precisely meeting the target reader's emotional needs

## The nine shock-scene ways

| Type | Method | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| Plain shock | event + one person shocked | a supporting character reacts to the protagonist's strength |
| Multi-person progression | A shocked → B shocked (B outranks A) | the small fry shocked first, the big shot more |
| Narration-plus | narration explains + characters shocked | build up the strike's brilliance, then the witnesses' reaction |
| Picture-plus | grand picture + characters shocked | heaven-and-earth effects, then kneeling |
| Self-contrast | the character shocked against their own past | "Is this the same demon ape from earlier?" |
| Rhetorical | self-questioning then shock | "I ask myself: could I do that? No way!" |
| Location-hopping | location 1 → 2 → 3 each shocked | the news spreading, multiple factions staggered |
| Body/mind split | body reaction + mental reaction | "chilled to the bone" + "mind shaken" in layers |
| Persona contrast | contempt first, shock after | "just this?" → "I must be dreaming" |

**Core**: shock's gratification comes from the "gap" — build the character's prior cognition (contempt/confidence), then break it.

### Three-fold-four-shock

```
Fold 1: the protagonist announces the choice → the crowd reacts
Fold 2: the specific content of the choice (surprising) → the crowd reacts again
Fold 3: probing the reason / a deeper reversal → a bigger reaction
Final shock: the authority's highest assessment → the loser exits dimmed (pressure release)
```

Each "fold" must out-shock the previous; every reversal must carry new information.

## Competition-scene prerequisites

Before writing a competition/duel, disclose to the reader:

1. **The stakes/cost**: what's won and lost
2. **The strength comparison**: at least imply the advantage/disadvantage
3. **The protagonist's win path**: at least one visible possible path
4. **The opponent's specialness**: why this battle deserves writing

### Win/loss design

| Mode | Difficulty | Effect | Points |
|------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Strong-beats-weak | low | best cost-efficiency | the information gap makes the gap; pair with flex |
| Even match | medium | strong tension | once per medium story, at the big climax |
| Weak-beats-strong | high | strongest gratification, riskiest | needs a sane explanation; no idiot-ball |

## The four-stage romance progression

```
Familiarity → testing → ambiguity → commitment
```

| Stage | State | Core writing | Forbidden |
|-------|-------|--------------|-----------|
| Familiarity | friends/strangers | show personas, build the first impression | no lover behavior |
| Testing | interested, unsure | testing behaviors, misunderstandings and sweetness | no direct confession |
| Ambiguity | more than friends, less than lovers | occasional lover behavior, blushing, racing hearts | female line: this stage must be stretched |
| Commitment | official couple | dog-food + the biggest obstacle event | no stepping back to testing |

**Iron rule**: do the right stage's things at the right stage. Skipping stages reads as greasy.

### The eight CP behavior types (appear only at the matching affection)

| Behavior type | Required affection |
|---------------|--------------------|
| Intimate behavior (physical contact and above) | high |
| Possessive behavior (jealousy, claiming) | medium-high |
| Trusting behavior (unconditional trust, secret-sharing) | medium+ |
| Protective behavior (defending them even when wrong) | medium+ |
| Special treatment (completely different from everyone else) | medium |
| Soothing behavior (noticing bad moods and soothing) | medium+ |
| Priority privilege (they matter most in decisions) | medium-high |
| Tacit rapport (doing the same thing unplanned) | high |

Affection must accumulate through plot; no out-of-thin-air level-skipping.

## Comedy flag mechanics

| Character | Flag rule |
|-----------|-----------|
| Protagonist | never collects flags, or collects them comically (not funny = poison point) |
| Antagonist | always collects flags (big talk must be paid with comeuppance) |
| Others | generally collect flags |

The antagonist's flags are payoff previews; the protagonist's flags are comedy tension — "I'll never..." followed by a comeuppance is funny.

## Short-fiction POV-control laws

### Character-count control
- The opening's main characters one-on-one or one-on-two; scenes use "small", not "large"
- In one-on-many, reduce supporting presence by importance
- Never a crowd entrance
- Supporting gazes and psychology center on the protagonist

### POV-count control
- Default: first-person victim, present throughout (the overwhelming majority of episodic shorts); other POVs only as brief regret/gloat inserts
- Multi-POV suspense exceptions: at most 2-3 POVs, never over 3
- Over 3 with each writing their own unconnected lines = follow-through drops
- Enemy POV: write it when it makes the reader clearer; skip it when it invites more guessing
- Only one enemy POV, occupying one of the three slots

## The three emotion layers and resonance triggers

### The three-layer separation

| Layer | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Character emotion | what the character feels |
| Text-conveyed emotion | what the text means to convey |
| Reader-felt emotion | what the reader actually feels |

Never conflate the three. One scene can have the character crying, the text flirting, and the reader gratified.

### Emotional anchors

**Definition**: concrete scenes that trigger the reader's inner resonance. Emotion touches the reader through anchors; it can't be piled up with direct description.

**Traits**: happens in the real world; commonly experienced or felt by most people; no fine description needed — a few key details suffice.

**Examples**:

| Emotion to convey | Weak writing | Good anchor |
|-------------------|--------------|-------------|
| A mother's love | mom cooks and cleans daily | mom secretly bows to every classmate, asking them to look after her blind child |
| Survival is hard | the protagonist digs roots and eats bark | everyone undercuts the price; the protagonist raises a hand: "I don't want money — just a meal" |
| Teacher-student bond | the teacher teaches diligently | the teacher gives the student their only coat and walks in the snow in shirtsleeves |

### Steel on the blade
1. Conceive the big plot first (largest contradiction → medium → small)
2. Place the finest anchors at the big plot's climax
3. Other parts may stay plainer
4. The emotional-setup process = the plot-advance process

**Design path**: contradiction → emotional demand → characters/background/setting → concrete scenes

## Antagonist mockery, new ideas

| Type | Traditional | New idea |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Purpose | poke the protagonist's wound, provoke discomfort | expose the antagonist's ignorance, provoke entertainment |
| Reader reaction | feel wronged for the protagonist | watch the joke and wait for the reversal |
| Protagonist response | rack their brain for a counter | relaxed, indifferent is enough |

**Operation**: determine the protagonist's advantage; let the antagonist mock that advantage to the max. The more the antagonist mocks, the more the reader sees their ignorance.
**Keep the wound-poking**: redirect it to the protagonist's friends and family — the antagonist attacks their weaknesses to provoke the reader's anger.

## Character decomposition from flaws

- Positive characters = virtues covering flaws
- Negative characters = flaws covering virtues
- Key element: the character's "unreconciled breath" — it must resonate

**Enrichment method**: take "decisive and ruthless" — positive = clean and quick; negative = no human warmth. Strengthen/weaken the "human warmth" to distinguish protagonist/supporting. A flesh-and-blood character = virtues and flaws coexisting.

## The small-beats-big module's four elements

1. **The protagonist's unique means**: why they can beat big with small
2. **The process of the bet**: the process, not the result, is the point
3. **The "scored big" feeling**: the gain far exceeds expectations
4. **A clear betting goal**: the protagonist must know what they want

## Payoff essence and the three cultivation realms

**Payoff essence** = changing relationships.

The three cultivation-realm essences: ant → dao-companion → senior. The protagonist advancing against small plot points creates imbalance and gratification. Small plot, small payoff; big plot, big payoff.

## Quality check

Check against these while writing:

- [ ] An info bomb in the first 100 words (not scenery/weather)?
- [ ] Every scene has at least two functions (advance + reveal / setup + raise)?
- [ ] Emotion touches the reader through anchors, not direct description?
- [ ] Shock scenes have a gap setup (build the character's prior cognition, then break it)?
- [ ] Romance advancement skips no stages (familiarity → testing → ambiguity → commitment)?
- [ ] Antagonist flags always collected; protagonist flags never or comically?
- [ ] POV control at 2-3 (never over 3)?
- [ ] Dialogue 50-70% of the body, every line advancing plot or revealing character?
- [ ] Endings close on action/dialogue (no elevation, no summary, no philosophizing)?
- [ ] No "should-gratify-but-doesn't" passages (cheat effects shown clearly)?
