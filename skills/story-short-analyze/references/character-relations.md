## Relationship types

Look up the type, then execute by its principles.

| Type | Definition | Function | Example |
|------|------------|----------|---------|
| Conflict | opposing interests/ideals | manufactures tension, advances plot | nemesis, competitor |
| Alliance | shared goals | provides help, builds bonds | comrade, mentor-student |
| Intimate | emotional bonds | creates soft spots, emotional anchors | lovers, family, brothers |
| Authority | superior/subordinate, domination | creates pressure, constrains the protagonist | mentor, boss, overseer |

**Execution rules**:
- Every important relationship gets at least one test (betrayal/sacrifice/misunderstanding)
- Relationships must have an arc (enemy to ally, ally to rival)
- Forbidden: every relationship "nice to each other", a solid block
- The relationship's function must serve the plot — never existing just to be sweet or miserable

## The romance persona-core method

In romance-driven fiction, the plot serves the persona — every plot must enrich the character or advance the romance, or it's invalid plot.

### Construction steps

1. **Fix the persona core**: write the character trait that first came to mind (e.g., "the prince who wants the throne", "the ailing prince")
2. **Diverge around the core**: answer — why this core? past experience? environmental influence? personality causes?
3. **Extend plot lines**: the core's inherent plots must be written (a goal → a goal line; a wound → a healing line)

### Execution rules

- Cliched plots with rich personas escape the cliche feel
- Both protagonists need bright spots and independent change paths; one shining while the other dims fails
- Highlights ≠ flex — every moment that makes the reader remember the character is a highlight, including the heart-wrenching ones
- Life beyond romance — the character wants love but their life can't be only love; a love-only character stands flat and thin

## Male-line vs female-line romance underlying logic

**Fix the target reader base first, then pick the matching logic. Mixing them makes readers feel "off".**

### Male-line romance logic

| Dimension | Notes |
|-----------|-------|
| Core | "external-factor display" — the couple together shows how excellent both are externally |
| Three core logics | "if only she were mine" → "this excellent person is mine" → "such an excellent person likes me, so I'm even more excellent" |
| Onlooker thought | "getting such an excellent woman — I envy him" (not "their love is sweet") |
| Heroine | ideally several women like the protagonist — the more, the more excellent he is |
| The partner's essence | a "trophy" — the ultimate purpose of everything is victory |

Write male-line romance events around "displaying excellence".

### Female-line romance logic

| Dimension | Notes |
|-----------|-------|
| Core | "connection" — a unique, unbreakable, top-priority emotional bond between the two |
| Connection requirement | "I love the person you are; appearance, wealth, and talent can't replace this bond" |
| Purity | after the bond forms, gradually shed external factors |
| Both sides | ideally first-love + both-only — guaranteeing the bond's purity and uniqueness |
| Onlooker thought | "their love is so sweet, I envy them" |

Write female-line romance events around "deepening the connection".

## Transmigration character-selection rules

### Mandatory conditions

- The narrative protagonist must be quite familiar with the original world (the core expectation source)
- Establish the familiar world in one or two lines; never more than a chapter before knowing where you are
- Pick a character with strong drama or contradiction after crossing (e.g., the villainess)

### Information-gap design

- Design information gaps on top of the original world for the protagonist's exploration space
- Original-world knowledge can exploit bugs for over-gains

## Love-triangle closing strategies

The love triangle is a contradiction at heart: control the confrontation intensity; no irreconcilable conflicts between romance-line parties.

| Closing method | Operation |
|----------------|----------|
| Link back to the career line | the parties convert jealousy into "who contributes more to the protagonist's career" |
| Insert a career crisis | at white heat, a sudden event switches everyone from infighting to united-against-the-outside |
| One-line pass | at most two people glaring and exchanging two taunts, very restrained |

## Sweet-naive character pitfalls

### The six pits to avoid

1. The heroine's mistakes must not be deliberate — never knowing-it's-wrong and doing it anyway
2. Her mistakes must not violate morality — the sweet-naive type's most important trait is childlike high morality
3. Never moralize from the high ground while failing to do it herself
4. Never have teammates apologize and praise her after her moralizing caused conflict
5. Correct version: higher moral standard → her deeds match it → she seems different and a little silly for it
6. A growth line is allowed: sitting in the seat of the person she scolded, she finds the old way was the best choice

### Reverse use

For a sweet-naive antagonist, pile all six onto her so readers loathe her on sight.

## The two-way flip of persona change

The persona-adjustment method when a romance-line relationship changes.

### Core idea

- The best change: both people change, and afterward the correspondence between them inverts exactly
- Change is a local adjustment of the original persona, tightly connected to it — no arbitrary remodeling

### Execution rules

- Persona changes happen after the "love-across-mountains" stage (especially after the biggest obstacle); don't design new conflicts — only feed sweetness
- The real obstacle was resolved earlier; now the reader's most urgent craving is more sugar
- Sugar is concrete CP behavior and careful tending — not "I love you" industrial syrup

## The character behavior-consistency check

### Step one: rule out external forcing

Check whether the character's behavior only serves pushing the plot. If the character "happens" to advance the plot without their own motive, mark "persona drift" and supplement a character-level sane reason for the behavior.

### Step two: confirm the emotional goal

Before each passage, fix three elements:
1. This passage's target emotion: what should the reader feel?
2. Does the character's personality support it: do the character's set attributes naturally lead this way?
3. Does the behavior fit the mission: is the character's behavior consistent with their functional positioning in the story?

### Step three: derive behavior from persona

Derive in-scene behavior from the character's set attributes (history/personality/goal/current state):
- List 2-3 possible reactions for the character in this scene
- Assess each against the character's existing behavior patterns
- Pick the most persona-consistent AND most dramatically tense one

### Step four: emotional-consistency check

Check whether the character's expressed emotion core matches the scene's target emotion. Mismatch → adjust the behavior or the scene target so the output is self-consistent on the emotional dimension.

**Two execution paths**:
- Path A: fix the target emotion → derive behavior from persona → check consistency
- Path B: derive behavior from persona → back-derive the scene target emotion → check consistency

## The conquest buffer zone

The supporting's attitude change toward the protagonist = the protagonist conquering the supporting, carrying huge anticipation and payoffs.

### Buffer-zone types

Online/offline, behind-the-back gossip, long-distance, status gaps, intimacy gaps, trust levels, information gaps, etc.

### Steps

1. Always keep the buffer zone present
2. Pick 5-7 event turning points from the volume outline
3. At each turning point, mark the supporting's state and attitude change toward the protagonist
4. At each key conquest point, the attitude change must be written clearly
5. The attitude change itself produces emotional waves and anticipation

### Execution rules

- Supporting aren't NPCs standing and waiting to be triggered
- Supporting have their own actions; events can be introduced from the supporting's viewpoint
- Same for positive characters: same-side people have their own actions and motives

## Identity-cognition gaps creating conflict

The same character's "standing" in different eyes is dynamic, not constant:

| Viewpoint | What matters | Assessment of the hero |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------|
| Worldly view | family parity, risk resistance | overall-condition match |
| The hero himself | breadwinning + love | relatively well-matched |
| The heroine | love + faithfulness | "if I care, it matches" |
| The rich second-gen | appearance/family match | "I'm the better match" |
| The passerby | overall comparison | "she should marry the rich one" |

**Points**:
- Different people's divergent assessments of the same character = natural conflict sources
- One of the romance genre's core payoffs: multi-dimension assessment gaps

## Frenemy relationships

The most magnetic relationship type.

### Execution rules

- Premise: both characters must be magnetic themselves — otherwise it's forced 50/50 glue
- Core: high mutual recognition + absolute conflict → respect between rivals → missing either, no charm
- A true nemesis = mutual recognition; an outsider's stamp doesn't count

## Competitor positioning and family lines

### The competitor (catfish effect)

- Positioning: adds pressure and urgency for the hero/heroine → pushes the conquest progress
- Plot placement: before the climax nodes → a setup-phase element
- Little use after the climax → not a life-support method

### The four family-line directions

| Direction | Function | Timing |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| His side, support | provides money/status/power | acts before the climax |
| His side, obstacle | obstacles the protagonist overcomes for recognition | usable before/after nodes |
| Her side, support | a warm supply station | acts before the climax |
| Her side, obstacle | parents' disapproval → striving for recognition | usable before/after nodes |

Support acts mainly before the climax; obstacles work before or after nodes — obstacles perform better.

## Male-line romance writing guide

### Why readers read romance fiction

| Value | Source |
|-------|--------|
| Emotional value | filling the "needed, cared-for" emotional vacancy |
| Self-esteem value | the heroine's excellence → showing her off → supporting envy |
| Why high-status heroines | a stronger flex tool + bigger self-esteem satisfaction |

### Core craft: write the romance like an escalation fiction

- The ambiguity-and-pull process is the most attractive part
- The affection progress bar: every step = one "level" in escalation fiction
- Every event is an "upgrade" → affection rises through conflict, resolution, understanding, and resonance

### The affection-upgrade path

Stranger → decent person (helps an old lady cross the street) → upright brave person (steps up) → good friend (understands the family situation) → likes-but-unsure (a special moment reveals the unseen side)

### Key principle: the protagonist doesn't pursue

- Male-line readers generally loathe "simp" personas
- Affection advances by the hero's own excellent qualities attracting, not by active pursuit
- The hero's help is a casual by-the-way — the heroine remembers him for it

### The asymmetry of advancement

- Two progress lines: surface social relationship (stranger → friend → couple) + actual emotional affection
- The two lines shouldn't advance in lockstep — one fast, one slow → rich conflicts
- Emotion faster than society → the "Kaguya mode" (affection maxed, both too proud to confess)
- Society faster than emotion → the "marriage-of-convenience mode" (surface couple, zero actual affection)

### Writing the heroine being good to him

| Heroine type | How she gives |
|--------------|---------------|
| Insecure introvert | secretly slipping things in, terrified of being found out |
| Tsundere | carefully made bento, but "I made too much, have some" |
| Straightforward | gives gifts openly or confesses directly |

### Use contrast well

| Contrast type | Operation |
|---------------|-----------|
| Timeline contrast | her attitude when they first met vs after knowing him |
| Double standard | no one else may touch her head, but he may → marks the special status |
| Information gap | the hero/heroine's double identity (online vs real life) → anticipation of the reveal reaction |

## The affection system (general framework)

### Four stages

Chance meeting → romance comedy → love across the mountains → the finale

### Affection × relationship-stage table

Affection (negative/zero/half/full) × relationship stage (familiarity/testing/ambiguity/commitment)

Stage-matching principle: **count by the lower side**. Affection reached but the stage not = abrupt behavior.

### The three CP behavior thresholds

| Behavior type | Threshold | Notes |
|---------------|-----------|-------|
| Tolerance-required behavior | half affection+ | active intimacy, compromise; must compensate or it reads as simping |
| Special-treatment behavior | half affection+ | possessiveness, trust, sacrifice, special treatment, soothing |
| Linked behavior | both half affection+ | tacit rapport, sharing, companionship |

Writing tolerance-required behavior at insufficient affection = greasy / harassment-feeling.

### Five romance-line structure templates

| Template | Core |
|----------|------|
| Affection change | may rise/fall/already-fallen → each has its path → aftershock |
| Benefit | enjoying the partner's perks = the romance flex core |
| Jealousy | multiple characters vying for the protagonist; the protagonist never vies for others |
| Blocked development | obstacle → attempted resolution → resolution → aftershock |
| Dog food | the couple's daily interaction |

## Character-goal independence and relationship-line design

### Protagonist-goal independence

- The protagonist's goal must be their own — not "helping someone else achieve theirs" — or the protagonist becomes a supporting/tool
- Correct: convert others' goals into the protagonist's own (suppressing a rebellion = protecting their own interests / gaining recognition / acquiring resources)
- Self-check: read one random chapter — what is the protagonist actively pursuing? If nothing, the protagonist has become a pawn

### Relationship-line layering

- Romance isn't linear; it has upgrade nodes, each matching a plot climax
- The social-line vs real-emotional-line mismatch creates tension
- Best rhythm: slow then fast — early setup accumulates affection, late concentrated eruption, matching a grand ritual

### Supporting functional positioning

| Type | Function | Timing |
|------|----------|--------|
| Competitor (catfish) | pressure driving the protagonist's action | before climax nodes |
| Supporting allies | resources/emotional support | acts before the climax |
| Obstacle allies | disapproval → conditions → the protagonist overcomes → recognition | usable before/after nodes |

---

## Green-tea / negative characters

- May advance the plot, but use with care
- Confirm the character's irreplaceability
- When the protagonist is strong, any character can become positive feedback
- When the protagonist is weak, negative characters get hated by readers

## Quality-check checklist

After each character-relationship/romance-line design, check item by item:

- [ ] **Relationship types clear**: every important relationship classified as conflict/alliance/intimate/authority
- [ ] **Relationships have arcs**: every important relationship experiences at least one test or change
- [ ] **Persona has a core**: the protagonist has a clear core trait and divergence basis
- [ ] **Goal independence**: the protagonist's goal is their own, not helping others achieve theirs
- [ ] **Affection matched**: CP behaviors match the current affection stage (count by the lower side)
- [ ] **Reader base aligned**: male-line events around "displaying excellence"; female-line around "deepening connection"
- [ ] **Supporting act**: not NPC-style standing and waiting; they have their own actions and motives
- [ ] **Buffer zone present**: the conquest process keeps the buffer zone; attitude changes marked at turning points
- [ ] **Love triangle controlled**: no irreconcilable conflicts between romance-line parties
- [ ] **Sugar timing right**: after love-across-the-mountains, no new conflicts; only concrete CP-behavior sugar
- [ ] **Life beyond romance**: the character's life contains more than romance; not a thin emotional tool
