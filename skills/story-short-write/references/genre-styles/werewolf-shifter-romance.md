# Genre Style Pack: Werewolf / Shifter Romance

> English short-form romance for GoodNovel / Dreame / Inkitt / Webnovel / Wattpad — the app-platform powerhouse of the romance market. Read `short-craft.md` first; this pack layers the genre-specific voice on top.
> One-line positioning: **alphas, mates, packs — the mate bond is destiny, and the story is the fight between what the wolf wants and what the human will choose.** The reader wants the claim, the scent, the pack politics, the rejection wound that gets healed by a stronger bond, and possessiveness that is protective instead of terrifying.

---

## Narrative voice

First person, female lead, present-tense-friendly per platform (GoodNovel/Dreame/Webnovel serials run close first person; Inkitt and Wattpad vary by heat tier). The voice runs on **scent and claim**: the narration tracks the bond physically — the pull in the chest, the mark, the heat of the change, the scent that announces a mate before the mind catches up. Nothing supernatural is narrated as metaphor; the wolf is felt in the body.

Dual track: the **human track** (choice, boundaries, self-respect, the parts of her life the wolf doesn't own) and the **wolf track** (instinct, claim, protection, the pull of the bond). The tension between the tracks is the genre's engine: the wolf wants, the human must choose, and the bond is a pull that she can refuse — the story is the choice, never the override. The mate bond is destiny in the world's law, but consent is the story's law: the claim only completes when she says yes.

## Opening patterns

A 120-220 word blurb opens with the wound or the claim in mid-motion + one line naming the bond's rule; the body's first section re-shoots the moment with texture.

| Opening type | Example |
|--------------|---------|
| The public rejection | "My mate rejected me at the pack gathering, in front of everyone, because a werewolf's mate is supposed to be a wolf. I'm human. I found out the same way the whole pack did — he said it out loud, and then he walked away. What he didn't know is that I heard the word 'forever' in his head when he said it, and the bond doesn't lie even when the wolf does." |
| The hidden mate | "The new alpha of the Silverclaw pack is my mate. He doesn't know it, because I've spent six years hiding my scent, and I've gotten very good at being invisible. Then he walked into my café, and the bond lit up like a struck match, and he stopped dead in the middle of the room and looked at me like I was the answer to a question he hadn't known he'd been asking." |
| The claimed human | "Humans aren't mates. That's the rule, and my wolf-mother reminded me of it on the day I presented as a wolf at all. What she didn't count on was the alpha of the neighboring pack catching my scent at the border — and what neither of us counted on was me running." |
| The wolf-less outcast | "I was born without a wolf. In my pack, that's worse than being born dead. Then the rogue alpha took the borderlands, and the prophecy everyone had forgotten said the mate of the true alpha would be the one who carried no wolf — because she'd carry the whole pack instead." |
| The rejected-mate return | "Five years ago, the alpha of the Bloodmoon pack rejected me for a wolf who was more 'suitable.' Tonight he's at my door, on his knees, and the scent of him says he knows exactly what he threw away. My wolf remembers. My human is holding the door open, and she's not sure which one of us is winning." |

## Hook motifs and density

The hook is **the bond and its tests**: every section either deepens the pull, raises the cost of refusing it, or lets the pack (and the reader) misread the bond. End each section on a claim interrupted, a secret scent caught, or a pack move.

- **The mate bond** (head motif): destiny that pulls — the scent, the ache, the mark. The bond is real and physical, but it is never the whole answer: the story's question is whether the human trusts it.
- **The rejection wound**: the bond refused, publicly and specifically. The wound is the engine of the rejection-to-second-chance story; its scar must be named and touched, not waved off by the new bond.
- **The scent reveal**: the mate scent is the genre's flash — the moment the other lead (or the pack) smells the truth. One per story at minimum, staged for maximum reaction.
- **The mark**: the claim's physical sign (the bite, the brand, the bond-thread). The mark is progress and public declaration at once; the pack reads it before the reader does.
- **Pack politics**: rank, challenge, the council of elders, the neighboring packs — the pack is the community that judges the bond; its tiers (the smug, the sorry, the silent) are the reaction shots.
- **The hidden mate**: one lead (or both) hides the bond — the hidden-scent years, the exile, the lie of the "wrong" mate. The reveal re-routes the prior friction.
- **The rogue threat**: the external danger (rogues, hunters, the war between packs) that forces the bond into the open and the alpha into protection mode.
- **The second-chance claim**: the rejected mate found by a stronger or truer bond — the new claim is chosen, never automatic; the old rejecter's regret is written in full but never wins.
- **The consent beat**: one scene per act where the human track states a boundary and the wolf track honors it — the genre's trust anchor and its market edge (see guardrails).

## Emotional intensity and patterns (intensity 8)

The engine is **hurt first, claim earned**: the rejection, the hidden years, or the pack's cruelty is the wound; the possessive care of the true mate is the healing — and the healing is earned in choices, not given by the bond. Emotion is named outright in the hurt register, then landed on a physical wolf-specific object (the mark, the scent, the ache, the fur).

- The hurt register: short, flat lines about the rejection's specifics (the audience, the words, the walk away). The wound is concrete, never "he didn't want me" abstraction.
- The wolf register: instinct stated plainly — the pull, the growl that means mine, the body moving before the mind decides. The wolf is never the villain of the story's ethics; it is the honest animal the human must teach.
- The claim register: possessive but safe — the protection scenes (the threat, the rescue, the standing-between) are the love language; jealousy is shown as defense of the bond, never isolation of her.
- The pack register: the community's pressure — the gossip, the ranking, the challenge — each scene a move in the hierarchy game.
- The low point: the old wound returns in public (the rejecter's apology or attack, the pack's verdict, the rogue's threat) and the human track must decide whether the new bond is trust or just another claim.
- The payoff: the consent beat completed — the mark taken by choice, the pack seeing the claim, the rejecter's face at the moment the new bond is declared. The choice only counts if refusing was possible.

Sample passage (register demonstration):

> The bond pulled at dawn, like a hand on the inside of my ribs, and I knew he was at the café before I opened the door. He was sitting at the corner table with his coffee untouched, and the whole room had the tilted quiet that follows him, the way a forest goes quiet for something large.
> "You've been hiding," he said.
> "I've been working." I poured his usual without asking, because the six years of hiding had at least taught me his order. "Alphas don't come to the café to talk about the weather."
> "No." He looked at me, and the pull in my chest went tight. "I came to ask why you've been hiding from me."
> "Maybe I don't like the way the bond talks."
> "It talks the truth." He said it like it was a law of nature. "You're my mate. You've been my mate for six years. You let me walk into this café every morning for a year without telling me."
> "You wouldn't have believed me," I said. "You'd have thought I was a trap. A human claiming to be an alpha's mate — the pack would have called it a spell."
> He was quiet for a long moment. Then he stood, and the room went quieter still.
> "The pack calls a lot of things spells," he said. "I don't take orders from the pack about this." He reached across the counter — slowly, like I was something that might startle — and touched the place over my heart where the bond had been pulling for six years. "I'd have believed you," he said. "I've been waiting for this scent my whole life."
> My wolf, who had been patient for six years, sat down inside me like a thing that had finally found its fire.

Second sample (the low point — the rejection's return, public):

> The Bloodmoon gathering was the first I'd attended since the rejection, and I stood at the edge of the firelight like a wolf who'd forgotten how to sit. He was there, of course — the alpha who'd said "not her" in front of everyone, five years ago — and he was looking at my neck, at the fresh mark, and the fury on his face was the color of the coals.
> "You found another mate," he said, loud enough for the whole gathering to hear. "A human's way of getting even."
> "I found my mate," I said. "He found me. The bond chose twice, and the second time it chose better."
> "The bond doesn't choose twice." He stepped into the firelight. "You've deceived him. You're not even a wolf —"
> "She's mine."
> The voice came from behind me, and the pack went quiet in the particular way it does when an alpha speaks without raising his voice. He walked past me, stopped in front of the Bloodmoon alpha, and I saw the thing every wolf in the gathering was pretending not to see: my mate was bigger, and he wasn't looking at the Bloodmoon alpha at all. He was looking at the mark on my neck.
> "She's mine," he said again. "The bond chose her, and she chose me, and neither of us needs the Bloodmoon's permission." He turned to me and held out his hand — not claiming, asking. "Come home."
> The fire crackled. The whole gathering watched. I took his hand, and the mark on my neck went warm, and I did not look back at the man who had once decided I was not enough. He was looking at me now, and that was the whole difference — everyone was looking at me now, and I was looking at the one who'd asked.

## Dialogue register and killer lines

Dialogue ratio 40-55%. The power marker is who speaks for the bond: the alpha's claim register is short, declarative, possessive-but-safe; the heroine's refusal register is plain and boundary-holding — her refusal is the reader's trust anchor.

**① The wounded-human register (armor lines)**
- "The bond doesn't obligate me. It offers. I'm allowed to refuse an offer."
- "I spent six years being invisible. I'm not spending the rest of my life being someone's proof."
- "You rejected me in front of the pack. I'm not accepting you in private."

**② The claim register (possessive, safe, short)**
- "You don't get to hide from me. You do get to run. I'll catch you either way — but I'd rather you stayed because you chose to."
- "I don't take orders from the pack about this." / "She's mine. She's been mine for six years. She just didn't know I knew."
- "The mark is mine to offer and yours to accept. Say no, and it stays an offer."

**③ The wolf-track register (instinct, plain)**
- "My wolf knew you before I did. He's not sorry about it. Neither am I."
- "The scent doesn't lie. It's the only thing in this world that doesn't."
- "I growl at people who get too close to you. I'm told that's not normal. I don't care."

**④ The consent register (the trust anchor)**
- "Say no, and I'll wait. I've waited six years. I can wait six more."
- "I'm not asking the bond to decide. I'm asking you. The bond is a door — you're the one who walks through it."

## Rhythm skeleton

```
Blurb (the wound or the claim + one line naming the bond's rule)
→ Section 1: the moment re-shot; the bond's rule stated in use
→ The wolf/human tracks established; pack pressure begins
→ The scent reveal (or the hidden mate's near-exposure)
→ The rejection's echo or the pack's verdict; the low point approaches
→ The rogue threat forces the bond public; the claim register peaks
→ The low point: the old wound returns publicly; the choice looms
→ The consent beat: the boundary stated, the choice made freely
→ The public claim + the callback revaluation (HE, concrete)
```

Dense in the scent reveal, the low point, and the public claim; sparse in the pack filler (one hierarchy detail per scene). The bond's rules must stay consistent — the pull, the mark, the refusal, and the consent are the story's physics; a bond that suddenly overrides choice is the genre's cardinal breach.

---

## Ending patterns

- **The completed mark**: the claim finished by choice — the mark taken, the bond sealed in a scene where the heroine says yes with the door open behind her.
- **The callback claim**: the ending returns the rejection (the gathering, the fire, the walk-away) and re-shoots it — this time claimed, this time in front of everyone, this time hers to choose.
- **The pack revalued**: the pack's verdict changes with the claim — the smug are quiet, the silent speak for her; the community's acceptance is shown, never announced.
- **The second-chance settle**: the rejecter's regret written once, in full, and answered once — the answer is the new bond, not revenge; no flip-flop after the claim.
- HE is a hard constraint: the bond completes, the couple ends together and safe, the pack (or the new pack) accepts. The rejecter does not win her back, but the ending never punishes her for moving on — the wound healed is the whole point.

## Genre-specific tuning (deviations from the general de-AI rules)

These read as AI-flavor elsewhere but are selling points here — **don't cut them**:

- **Physical bond language**: keep the pull, the ache, the scent, the mark — written as body, never metaphor. Rotate the image per scene; the same ache twice in a row reads as recycled.
- **Direct emotion words in the hurt register**: keep the rejection's specifics ("he said 'not her' in front of everyone"); land them on the scene's concrete objects (the firelight, the mark, the open door).
- **The claim register's possessiveness**: keep the protective, safe possessiveness; the possessive line must always be paired with a consent line or a shown boundary — possessive without choice is the genre's abuse-adjacent failure mode.
- **The wolf as honest animal**: keep the wolf's plain instinct lines; the wolf is not an excuse and never overrides the human's refusal.
- **Pack reaction shots**: keep the tiers (the smug, the sorry, the silent); the pack is the audience that makes the claim public.

Still avoid the genuine AI-flavor list: "the kind of X that Y", "a wave of X washed over", "in that moment", "he finally understood", "fate had other plans", "his eyes were like molten gold" (one concrete wrong/right detail instead), emotion-by-summary instead of behavior, and the abusive-alpha caricature (a controlling, isolating lead with no shown safety reads as lazy and fails the platform lines below).

## Platform content-line guardrails (explicit heat content safety)

These are the pack's bright lines — violating them gets stories pulled and is a genuine market risk on app platforms:

- **Heat tiers by platform**: Wattpad / Tapas — tasteful-to-implied; the bond's physical claim stays closed-door or fades before the explicit act. GoodNovel / Dreame / Webnovel — moderate heat by default: kissing, marking (the bite), claiming scenes implied; check each platform's current policy before writing on-page acts. Inkitt / Radish / Galatea — explicit allowed only where the platform's mature tier permits; mark the heat tier in the plan so the reader's expectation matches the content.
- **Consent is the genre's contract**: the mate bond is a pull, never an override. Every claim scene must contain a choice the heroine makes with full information — "the wolf took over" is a banned line. Coercion, non-consent, or dub-con framed as destiny is the genre's cardinal violation and a bright-line content breach on every app platform.
- **The possessive register stays protective**: jealousy and claim are shown as defense of the bond (the threat, the rescue, the standing-between) — never as isolation, surveillance, or control of her choices. If the alpha restricts her agency, that's abuse-adjacent content, not heat.
- **No underage content**: leads and pack members are adults (18+); any school or academy setting ages everyone up explicitly.
- **No bestiality framing**: the shifter is a person in both forms; the wolf form is never a sexual participant, and heat scenes happen in human (or shifted-human) form with full personhood maintained.
- **No violence as titillation**: pack challenges, rogue fights, and the rejection wound stay on the emotional and physical-plot side; graphic injury or gore is never paired with the romance's heat. The rejection's pain is emotional, and the pack's cruelty is shown as wrong, never celebrated.

## Structural formulas (by length)

- **The rejected mate (8-10 sections)**: 1-2 the public rejection + the wound's specifics → 3 the leaving (or the hidden years) → 4 the new bond's first signal (the scent) → 5 the rejecter's regret surfaces (eavesdropped or attempted apology) → 6 the low point: the old wound returns publicly, the choice looms → 7 the consent beat: the new claim offered, freely accepted → 8-9 the public claim + the rejecter's full regret answered once → 10 the settled pack + the callback revaluation. First person; the alpha's POV optional as one planted chapter. See Formula 11 in `genre-writing-formulas.md` for the beat table.
- **The hidden mate (8-11 sections)**: the hidden-scent years / the exile frame → the near-exposure (twice) → the reveal (the scent caught, the lie broken) → the pack's verdict → the rogue threat forces the bond public → the claim + the callback. The reveal is the midpoint, not the ending — the story after the reveal is the pack's judgment and the choice.
- **The wolf-less outcast (8-11 sections)**: the no-wolf wound → the prophecy or the true alpha's arrival → the pack's contempt vs the alpha's notice → the border threat → the identity reveal (she is the one the prophecy meant) → the public claim, chosen → the pack revalued. The subversion option: the prophecy is false, and the pack accepts her anyway — acceptance earned, not foretold.
