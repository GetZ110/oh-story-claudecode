# Genre Style Pack: Why-Choose (Reverse Harem)

> English short-form romance for Wattpad / Tapas / Inkitt / Galatea / Dreame (mature sections where allowed). Read `short-craft.md` first; this pack layers the genre-specific voice on top.
> One-line positioning: **one lead, multiple love interests, and a bond that grows by addition rather than elimination — the fantasy is being wanted completely, by different kinds of wanting, and never being forced to choose the wrong one.** The reader wants each love interest to be a full, distinct person, the jealousy to be managed and eventually shared, and the ending where nobody is left behind.

---

## Narrative voice

First person (the shared lead) on Wattpad/Tapas/Dreame; close third or first per Inkitt/Galatea. The voice runs on **differentiation**: the narration tracks each love interest with a distinct internal register — the way she thinks about each one is as different as the way they speak. The reader must always know whose scene it is even with names removed.

Structure note: this is the pack where the "one couple" rules of the other romance packs deliberately do not apply. The genre's identity is the ensemble bond — the love interests form a unit around the lead, and the romance is the unit's formation. Each love interest gets: a distinct voice, a distinct want from the relationship, a distinct cost he's willing to pay, and his own scene of being chosen (or choosing) — no one is decoration.

---

## Opening patterns

A 120-220 word blurb opens with the premise + the cast's first collision; the body's first section establishes the frame that gathers them.

| Opening type | Example |
|--------------|---------|
| The inheritance | "My grandmother left me a ranch, a debt, and a standing agreement with three families: they'd work the land, and I'd choose the one who married me. The catch was in the fine print — the agreement didn't say 'one.'" |
| The academy | "The academy admitted four people to the dragon-bonding program every century. I was the first from our village. The other three were princes, heirs, and the single most insufferable genius in the kingdom, and the bond chose me before the term began. All of it." |
| The crew | "The crew took me in when the city had no use for me: the captain who never asks twice, the doctor who says too little, the pilot who says too much. They were a unit before I arrived. The problem is the unit keeps making room." |
| The pack | "The pack's law was simple: one wolf, one mate, forever. I was human, bitten, and a liability. They argued about the law for three days. Then the alpha asked if I'd consider the pack the way the pack considered me — together." |
| The court | "The queen sent her three sons to court me: the general, the scholar, the jester. It was a test of which alliance our kingdom would buy. Nobody warned me the test was mutual, and that the boys had started placing bets on which of them I'd break first. The joke was on all of them." |

## Hook motifs and density

The hook is **the unit forming**: every section either adds one love interest's depth, advances one relationship track, or forces a convergence (the mission, the threat, the bonding ceremony). End sections on a unit-formation beat (a shared secret, a loyalty choice, a public declaration).

- **The frame** (head motif): the story needs a legitimate frame that gathers them (the ranch agreement, the academy cohort, the crew, the pack law, the court test, the prophecy) — the frame is the premise's spine; without it, the story is a love-interest catalogue.
- **The distinct want**: each love interest wants something different from the lead (protection, peace, chaos, legacy, understanding) — their wants must conflict with each other at least once (the jealousy beat) and resolve into shared terms (the unit beat).
- **The rotation principle**: each relationship track advances in rotation — one love interest's beat per section, never two in a row — so every reader's favorite gets fed; two sections without a given interest's beat is a dropped thread.
- **The jealousy beat**: managed, not avoided — one scene per act where two interests collide over her; the collision must end in a resolution that widens rather than narrows (they agree to share the watch, the mission, the risk).
- **The convergence threat**: the plot forces them together (the hunt, the siege, the bonding ceremony, the enemy) — the threat is the unit's welder; each act ends with the unit acting as one.
- **The chosen-not-ranked moment**: each love interest gets one scene of being explicitly chosen (the rescue she runs to him, the secret she tells him first, the touch she allows only him) — the reader must see every bond as real and chosen, not a harem-by-default.
- **The unit beat**: the scene where the unit becomes a unit — the shared vow, the first joint fight, the agreement to share her time — is the genre's midpoint climax; after it, the jealousy register converts to protection register.
- **The external antagonist**: the villain is never one of the love interests (they can be rivals, never enemies); the threat comes from outside the unit (the family, the law, the enemy court, the hunter).

## Emotional intensity and patterns (intensity 7)

The engine is **abundance with fairness**: the reader wants every bond to feel complete and none to feel cheated. Emotion is named and landed on per-interest objects (the captain's compass, the doctor's clean hands, the pilot's star charts).

- The differentiation register: each love interest's scenes carry his own texture — the way she moves in his space, the silence between them, the argument rhythm. If a scene could be swapped between two interests with no change, it's a dropped-character scene; rewrite.
- The want register: each interest's want is named once, early, and then shown through his acts (the captain who waits at the door, the doctor who learns her allergies, the pilot who charts the route home).
- The jealousy register: kept short and resolved within the act — jealousy that drags three sections becomes a dragged plot.
- The conversion register: after the unit beat, the jealousy converts to protection — the interests compete to protect her, then to protect each other; the comedy of the over-protective unit is a genre signature.
- The low point: the external threat targets the unit (the family's ultimatum, the law, the enemy taking one of them) — the lead must choose between saving the unit and saving her own name; the choice is the sacrifice beat.
- The payoff: the unit stands, public (the declaration, the ceremony, the court's defeat) — each interest gets his line, his act, his place; the ending is the family portrait.

Sample passage (register demonstration):

> The captain was first at the door, of course. He was always first at doors. It was a whole thing.
> "You took the pilot's route again," I said.
> "The pilot takes the long way to spend time with you." He set the box on the table — my grandmother's ledgers, the ones I'd mentioned once, in passing, at breakfast. "The ledger's the short way."
> The doctor came in behind him, said nothing, and put a cup of tea exactly where my hand would land. He'd learned that from watching. I'd caught him watching. He'd said, "Observation is a medical practice," and I'd let him have it.
> The pilot arrived last, with the noise: "She's been up three hours. The sunrise route was worth it. I have proof — I drew it."
> "You drew the sunrise," the captain said.
> "I drew her watching the sunrise. There's a difference. It's called art."
> "It's called a stalker."
> "It's called—" The pilot stopped, because I was laughing, and the doctor was almost smiling, and the captain's mouth had gone crooked at the corner, which was his version of losing.
> "I have a lot of work," I said. "Three of you is a lot of work."
> "We know," the captain said. "We're splitting the load."

Second sample (the unit beat — the watch rotation):

> The enemy hit at midnight, which was the pilot's hour, which meant the pilot was already awake and already furious. The alarm went up, and the house became a ship: the captain at the door with the ledger of exits, the doctor at the back with the kit he packed for three, the pilot on the roof with the route he'd drawn for four.
> I stood in the middle of it, and the strange thing was that I wasn't scared. Not the way I'd been scared my whole life, alone in rooms that were supposed to be safe.
> "Watch your left flank," the captain said, to me, of all people — as if I were a unit now. As if the unit had always been four.
> "She doesn't fight," the doctor said, which was a correction, not an insult.
> "She doesn't need to," the pilot said, from the roof. "She's the reason we're all still here. That's the watch."
> The enemy came and went, and in the morning the house was still standing, and the captain said the rotations would be posted, and the doctor said the tea would be hot, and the pilot said he'd drawn the sunrise again, and I looked at the three of them and understood, for the first time, what the pack law meant when it said 'together'.
> It meant the watch never comes alone.

## Dialogue register and killer lines

Dialogue ratio 45-60%. The power marker is the unit's internal law: they negotiate each other as much as they negotiate with her — the interests' dialogue is a standing committee meeting, and their bickering is the genre's comedy and its love language.

**① The distinct voices (each interest, unmistakable)**
- The captain (short, command-register): "I'm not asking. I'm posting the watch."
- The doctor (sparse, precise): "You'll take the dose. The dose is not negotiable. The flavor is negotiable."
- The pilot (long, enthusiastic): "I've charted four routes to the festival, three of which pass things you'll like, and one of which passes a pie stand I have been told is essential, so really the question is how much pie we're willing to be."
- The scholar (dry, quoted): "The treaty says the alliance is binding. The treaty says nothing about the bed. I have read the treaty. I have also read it aloud to the general. He objected. I cited."

**② The committee register (the unit negotiating)**
- "You took her the ledgers." / "She mentioned them." / "She mentions the stars to me." / "The stars are slow. The ledgers are practical." / "You're both wrong. The tea is what did it." — (the doctor, winning by silence)
- "If you two are going to fight over the watch, I'll take it." / "You're on the watch." / "We're all on the watch. She's going to find out." / "She already knows. She's the only one who knows anything."

**③ The chosen-beat register (each bond, explicit)**
- To the captain: "You came to the door first. I keep noticing you come first. It's not about the door."
- To the doctor: "You know my allergies, my habits, my tea. That's not observation. That's a courtship."
- To the pilot: "You drew the sunrise. I want to see it every day. That's a contract."

**④ The unit register (the payoff)**
- "You argued for three days about the law. Then you asked me to consider you together. No one has ever asked me to be considered as a whole. I'm saying yes to the whole."
- "The court wanted one alliance. They're getting a fleet."

## Rhythm skeleton

```
Blurb (premise + cast's first collision)
→ Section 1: the frame establishes; all interests introduced with distinct voices
→ Rotation cycles: one interest's beat per section; wants named
→ First jealousy beat (resolved wider); the frame's pressure arrives
→ Second rotation; the chosen-not-ranked moments (one per interest)
→ The unit beat (midpoint): jealousy converts to protection
→ The external threat targets the unit; the low point
→ The sacrifice beat: she chooses the unit, the unit chooses her
→ The payoff: public declaration + family portrait
```

Dense in each interest's chosen-beat scene and the unit beat; sparse in the committee-comedy transitions (one bickering line per scene is enough — a full comedy scene per section kills the rotation). Track the rotation in `setting.md`: every love interest must get his beat at least every 2-3 sections.

## Ending patterns

- **The family portrait**: the ending gathers the unit, public (the declaration, the ceremony, the court) — each interest gets his line and his place; the last image is the whole family, no one missing.
- **The standing committee**: the ending is domestic — the unit's bickering over dinner, the watch rotation, the shared house — the genre's settled fantasy.
- **The bonded ceremony**: the frame's ritual (the pack's law, the academy's bond, the treaty's marriage) performed with all of them — the world's rule ratified the unit.
- **The threshold scene**: the final image is the unit at a threshold (the door, the gate, the new land) — callback to the opening frame, revalued, everyone in the frame.
- HE is a hard constraint: the unit ends together, everyone chosen, no one sacrificed or dropped. A love interest can grieve or grow, but cannot be cut from the ending — a dropped interest is the genre's cardinal sin and its reader-review killer.

## Genre-specific tuning (deviations from the general de-AI rules)

These read as AI-flavor elsewhere but are selling points here — **don't cut them**:

- **Distinct internal registers per interest**: keep the per-interest narration texture; a scene swappable between two interests is a rewrite signal, not a style choice.
- **Committee bickering**: keep the unit's negotiation comedy; it's the genre's warmth engine. Keep it short per scene.
- **The rotation**: keep the per-section rotation discipline; feeding one interest for two sections starves another reader.
- **The chosen-not-ranked moments**: keep each explicit chosen-beat; the reader's favorite must see herself chosen.
- **The conversion arc**: keep jealousy→protection as a visible arc; jealousy that persists past the unit beat reads as a dragged plot.
- **The family-portrait endings**: keep the everyone-gets-a-line closure; the genre's satisfaction is structural.

Still avoid the genuine AI-flavor list: "the kind of X that Y", "a wave of X washed over", "his jaw tightened" repeated per interest (differentiate the body language), "in that moment", "she felt a flutter of something" (name the something, per interest), and interchangeable love interests — same voice, same want, same line. **Also avoid**: jealousy as a permanent state (the genre resolves it), a love interest who exists only to be jealous (every interest needs his own scenes with her), and ranking-by-elimination — this genre does not eliminate; if you want a single-couple elimination structure, that's enemies-to-lovers or dark-romance territory.

## Structural formulas (by length)

- **The ensemble frame (9-12 sections)**: 1-2 frame + all interests introduced with distinct voices → 3-5 rotation cycles + wants named, first jealousy beat → 6 the chosen-not-ranked moments (one per interest) → 7 the unit beat → 8-9 external threat + sacrifice beat → 10-11 payoff + family portrait. First person; the rotation tracked in setting.md.
- **The academy/bonding (9-12 sections)**: the cohort frame → the trial structure (each section one trial that tests one bond) → the convergence trial (the unit welded) → the enemy revealed as outside → the final trial + bonded ceremony. Fantasy flavor optional; mechanics from fantasy-romance.md apply to the magic.
- **The court test (10-13 sections)**: the queen's test frame → the court's scheme as the antagonist → the interests' real characters revealed against the court's expectations → the jealousy beat as a court-engineered trap → the unit beats the court with its own rules → the public declaration. Merges with fantasy-romance.md's court register; the romance mechanics stay here.
