# Genre Style Pack: Domestic Thriller

> English short-form thriller for Kindle (KU short reads; Kindle top-20 strength) / Inkitt / Radish / Galatea serials. Read `short-craft.md` first; this pack layers the genre-specific voice on top.
> One-line positioning: **the danger is inside the house — the marriage, the family, the picket fence — where the reader's own life is the horror: gaslighting, secrets, the spouse you married, the twist that makes the re-read a different book.** The reader wants the dread of the familiar, the unreliable truth, and the ending that re-routes everything they just trusted.

---

## Narrative voice

First person, female lead (the wife, the mother, the widow) per the Verity model; close third or multi-POV for Inkitt/Galatea thriller serials. The voice runs on **the gap between what's said and what's seen**: the narration is the character's testimony, and the twist retroactively judges it. The narrator's perception may be warped — by gaslighting, by grief, by her own design — and the reader must be able to find, on re-read, exactly where the truth bent. Unreliability is engineered, never vague: every scene carries at least one detail that reads one way and re-reads another.

Dual track: the **surface track** (the perfect life narrated as it's presented: the husband, the house, the anniversary) and the **evidence track** (the object, the habit, the small wrongness that accumulates — the journal, the camera, the timeline). The two tracks are the story: the surface is what she's told to believe, the evidence is what she sees, and the twist decides which one was the lie.

## Opening patterns

A 120-220 word blurb opens with the wrongness in mid-motion + one ordinary detail that will re-read as the twist's seed; the body's first section re-shoots the wrongness with texture.

| Opening type | Example |
|--------------|---------|
| The found journal | "My husband doesn't know I found the journal. It's not his — it's the previous wife's, the one who 'died tragically' in the boating accident everyone loved telling me about. The last entry is dated the day she died, and it says: 'He's reading my journal. He's been reading it for months. I have to make him think I don't suspect.'" |
| The perfect husband | "Everyone tells me how lucky I am. The house, the ring, the husband who remembers anniversaries. Yesterday I found the anniversary gift two weeks early, wrapped in the back of his closet, and the card inside was addressed to a name that isn't mine — and the handwriting was his, and the date was last year." |
| The house's history | "We moved in six months ago. The previous owners left in a hurry — I found the half-packed boxes in the attic, the photo album, and the police report they'd forgotten to take with them. My husband says I'm imagining the pattern. I'm not imagining the pattern. The pattern is that every wife on this street leaves within two years." |
| The gaslight | "The medicine was on the counter. Now it's not. My husband says I took it, and I have a hole in my memory where taking it should be, and that's the thing — he's always there, gently, helpfully, filling in the gaps in my mind. The gaps are getting bigger. I started writing everything down, and then the notebook disappeared, and he said — kindly, patiently — that I never had a notebook." |
| The new neighbor | "The new neighbor knows things about my husband she shouldn't know. I know this because I'm the one who gave her the details — I've been feeding her the truth for a month, in the garden, where the cameras can't reach. She thinks she's investigating him. She doesn't know I'm investigating her." |

## Hook motifs and density

The hook is **the evidence and its reliability**: every section either adds a piece of evidence, raises a doubt about the evidence already gathered, or re-reads an earlier scene. End each section on a detail that refuses to fit, a disappeared object, or a question about the narrator's own memory.

- **The gaslight** (head motif): the gentle correction, the misplaced object, the memory hole — the spouse's tool, shown as harm. Each gaslight move is a scene; the reader tracks the pattern the way the narrator can't.
- **The perfect surface**: the house, the ring, the anniversary — the surface narrated warm at first; the twist re-reads every warm scene as staged.
- **The previous wife**: the dead predecessor (or the ex, the missing, the divorced) whose story rhymes with the narrator's — the genre's warning system; the rhyme is the escalation.
- **The house with a history**: the attic boxes, the police report, the neighbors' patterns — the place itself as evidence.
- **The surveillance**: the cameras, the phone, the location history — who watches whom is the genre's power map; the watcher's POV flips at the twist.
- **The disappeared evidence**: the notebook, the pill bottle, the recording — evidence that vanishes proves the threat and indicts the memory; each disappearance is a scene with a witness (the empty drawer, the changed lock).
- **The unreliable narrator**: the narrator's testimony may be warped by design, grief, or gaslighting — every warped scene plants a re-readable seed (the date, the tone, the thing she "remembers" wrong).
- **The ally**: the neighbor, the sister, the old friend who confirms the pattern — the ally's reliability is itself a question; a false ally is the double-reversal's engine.
- **The staged accident**: the boating accident, the fall, the fire — the genre's threat vocabulary; the accident's mechanics are the plot's puzzle.
- **The twist's re-read**: the ending reveals which scenes were true; the genre's contract is that flipping back finds the seams, never contradictions.

## Emotional intensity and patterns (intensity 8)

The engine is **dread by accumulation**: every section converts the ordinary into the ominous — the familiar is the horror, and the escalation is one small wrongness at a time. Emotion is named sparingly and landed on the evidence (the empty drawer, the changed lock, the card addressed to another name).

- The surface register: warm, grateful, deliberately calm — the narration of the life as presented; the reader learns to read it as testimony, not truth.
- The doubt register: the small uncertainty — the misplaced object, the skipped memory — written flat and concrete; the reader's dread grows faster than the narrator's.
- The dread register: once the pattern is seen, the ordinary details turn — the husband's kindness reads as control; the kindness scenes must be re-readable both ways.
- The evidence register: the collection scenes — the journal, the camera, the timeline — written with the procedural care of the thriller register.
- The low point: the narrator's own mind breaks trust — the memory hole, the false accusation, the moment the reader doubts her too.
- The payoff: the twist — the reveal that re-routes the testimony — landing fast, flat, and complete (see reversal timing rules); the re-read begins immediately in the same section.

Sample passage (register demonstration — the gaslight escalates):

> The pill bottle was where I left it. I knew this the way I knew the kitchen's light switch and the creak in the third stair — certainty, muscle memory, the body's filing system. Then I walked back from the sink and it was not where I left it. It was on the other side of the counter, next to his coffee cup.
> "You moved my pills," I said.
> "I didn't touch them." He didn't look up from his phone. "You set them down when you were making tea. You were tired."
> "I wasn't tired."
> He looked up then, and his face did the thing I'd stopped being able to name: patient, kind, a little concerned, the face of a man watching a woman he loved struggle with something he couldn't fix. "You were," he said, gently. "You mentioned it at dinner. You said the day had worn you out."
> I had not mentioned it at dinner. I had not said anything at dinner except that the soup was good, and I remembered that the way I remembered the pill bottle's position — certainty, muscle memory. But he said it with such kindness, and the kitchen was so warm, and the bottle was so clearly on the other side of the counter, that for a second I believed him.
> That second was the gap.
> I wrote it down that night, in the notebook I'd started, the one he didn't know about: moved my pills, claims I said I was tired. And then I wrote the date, and then I looked at the date, and then I sat very still, because the notebook from last week, the one with the same entries, was gone — and I had not thrown it away, and I had not forgotten it, and the drawer where I kept it was empty, and the drawer's lock was new.

Second sample (the twist lands — the re-read begins):

> I found the second phone in the garden shed, under the potting bench, in the toolbox no one used. It was his. It had been his for two years. And the files on it were mine — all of it, the notebooks I'd hidden, the photos I'd taken, the letters I'd written to myself in the worst weeks, everything I'd believed was private.
> So I went back through the two years, the way you go through a house after a flood, and I found the seams. The neighbor who'd "confirmed" my husband's alibi was his cousin. The therapist I'd seen after the first memory hole was the man who'd married my sister. The accident I'd been so afraid of — the boating accident, the one that had taken the previous wife — had a police report that named a witness, and the witness was a name I knew, and the name was mine, and I had never been on that boat.
> I sat on the potting bench with the phone in my hands and the garden going gold outside, and I understood, with the clarity of a person who has just been handed the missing page, that I had not been losing my mind for two years.
> I had been told I was losing my mind.
> There's a difference. It's the difference between drowning and being held under. The surface looks the same, but one of them has hands.
> I put the phone in my pocket, went inside, and made him tea. His favorite kind. He looked up from the paper and smiled his patient, kind, concerned smile, the one I'd stopped being able to name.
> "You look better today," he said.
> "I slept well," I said. And I had — for the first time in two years, I had slept like the dead, because I finally knew what I was dealing with, and the dead, as it turned out, sleep very well indeed.

## Dialogue register and killer lines

Dialogue ratio 35-45% — the genre breathes through narration, and dialogue is where the gaslight lives. The power marker is who corrects whom: the gaslighter's lines are patient, reasonable, slightly corrective; the narrator's lines grow shorter and less certain; the reveal register is flat and complete.

**① The gaslight register (patient, corrective, re-readable)**
- "You must have forgotten. You've been under so much strain."
- "I never said that, love. You're conflating it with last week."
- "Maybe we should see someone about the memory thing. I worry about you. That's all this is — worry."
- "You're exhausted. You should rest. I'll handle the pills."

**② The doubt register (the narrator, growing shorter)**
- "I didn't forget. I set it down right here."
- "Then where is it?" (Silence.)
- "You're sure you said that?" — the line the narrator practices and can't land.

**③ The evidence register (the procedural voice)**
- "I found the date. I found the receipt. I found the name you thought I'd never see."
- "The notebook is gone. The drawer is empty. The lock is new. That's a pattern, and patterns are evidence."
- "You didn't take my memory. You borrowed it. Now I want it back."

**④ The reveal register (flat, complete, one pass)**
- "I wasn't losing my mind. I was being handed a map of it."
- "You were very good at being the only person who believed me. That's the tell."
- "The accident had a witness. The witness was me. I was never on that boat — but you knew that."

## Rhythm skeleton

```
Blurb (the wrongness in mid-motion + one detail that will re-read as the twist's seed)
→ Section 1: the wrongness re-shot; the surface register and the evidence register both open
→ The gaslight moves, one per section; the doubt register grows
→ The evidence accumulates (the journal, the camera, the timeline); the ally appears
→ The disappeared evidence: the pattern is seen; the dread register peaks
→ The low point: the narrator's own memory indicts her (the false accusation, the hole)
→ The ally's reliability cracks (the planted confirmation)
→ The twist: the reveal lands, flat and complete; the re-read begins immediately
→ The escape/exposure + the callback to the opening wrongness, revalued
```

Dense in the gaslight scenes, the evidence scenes, and the twist; sparse in the surface-life filler (one perfect detail per scene, each re-readable). Every gaslight scene must be re-readable as coercion after the twist — the seams are the genre's contract.

## Ending patterns

- **The escape**: the narrator acts — the evidence secured, the exit taken, the exposure delivered; the ending shows her out, whole, with the proof in hand. The escape is concrete (the car, the police station, the lawyer's office), never vague.
- **The exposure**: the twist delivered to the world — the second phone, the report, the audience; the gaslighter's tiered collapse (denial, bluster, silence) in one scene.
- **The re-read revalued**: the ending returns the opening image (the pill bottle, the anniversary, the journal) — re-shot with the truth, the warmth revealed as staging.
- **The subversion twist**: the narrator was the manipulator all along — the re-read revalues her testimony as craft; used once per story, never telegraphed, and the ending stays within the story's own ethics (the harm is still named as harm).
- Domestic thrillers permit non-HE endings by genre; the ending's contract is resolution — the abuse ends (escape, exposure, justice, or the narrator's own accounting). The abuse is never romanticized, and the narrator's survival is the residue the reader carries.

## Genre-specific tuning (deviations from the general de-AI rules)

These read as AI-flavor elsewhere but are selling points here — **don't cut them**:

- **Re-readable testimony**: keep the narration as testimony with planted seams (the date, the tone, the misattributed memory); every seam must be findable on flip-back. The re-read is the genre's product.
- **The gaslight's kindness**: keep the patient, reasonable, corrective surface of the abuser's lines — the kindness is the tool; a shouty villain gives the reader the relief of certainty too early.
- **The ordinary as ominous**: keep the small, ordinary wrongness (the moved bottle, the new lock, the misdated memory); the escalation is accumulation, never one loud event.
- **The procedural evidence voice**: keep the collection scenes' concrete mechanics (dates, receipts, timelines); the evidence is the genre's suspense engine.
- **Flat reveal prose**: keep the twist delivered flat, fast, and complete — no melodrama at the reveal; the reader's work at the reveal is the re-read, not the gasp.

Still avoid the genuine AI-flavor list: "the kind of X that Y", "a chill ran down her spine" (show the detail that chills), "in that moment", "she finally understood" (the understanding is the reveal scene, shown), "everything was about to change", "fate had other plans", emotion-by-summary instead of behavior, and the tell-all diary entry that dumps the whole twist at once — the twist surfaces through evidence, never an author's note.

## Platform content-line guardrails

Domestic thrillers traffic in abuse-adjacent material; the content lines keep the story on-platform and ethical:

- **On-page violence minimal**: the genre's threat is psychological — the gaslight, the surveillance, the staged accident's aftermath. Graphic violence, gore, or prolonged physical-abuse scenes are off-register; the violence stays off-page or aftermath-only, and never becomes the story's spectacle.
- **No violence against children on-page**: family-secret plots keep any child harm off-page and aftermath-only; the child's safety is the story's line.
- **Gaslighting is shown as harm**: the manipulation is never romanticized or framed as love; the narrator's pain is the story's truth anchor, and the abuser's "he did it because he loved her" framing is a banned rationalization.
- **The ending resolves the abuse**: escape, exposure, or justice — the abuse ends; an ending that returns the narrator to the abuser, or frames the abuse as acceptable, is a bright-line breach and a market failure on KU and app platforms.
- **Trigger-awareness in the blurb**: suicide-adjacent, addiction, or self-harm motifs (if used) are flagged with care in the plan; the story's resolution never leans on a cheap "she died anyway" twist after trauma — the twist must stay within the story's own evidence.

## Structural formulas (by length)

- **The gaslight reveal (8-10 sections)**: 1-2 the perfect surface + the first wrongness (re-readable seed) → 3-4 the gaslight moves, one per section; the doubt register grows → 5 the evidence accumulates; the ally appears → 6 the disappeared evidence; the pattern is seen → 7 the low point: the narrator's memory indicts her → 8 the ally's reliability cracks → 9 the twist lands (70-85%), flat and complete → 10 the escape/exposure + the callback revalued. First person, unreliable-by-design. See Formula 13 in `genre-writing-formulas.md` for the beat table. High-info-density: 300+ words/section permitted.
- **The previous-wife frame (9-11 sections)**: the journal/frame as the engine → the rhymes accumulate (each section one parallel) → the narrator's own safety erodes → the ally revealed as planted → the twist (the accident's witness, the timeline) → the escape. The frame's final entry is answered in the final section.
- **The surveillance variant (8-10 sections)**: the watcher POV as the opening cold scene → the narrator's counter-surveillance → the power map flips at the twist (the watcher was watched, or the watched was the watcher) → the exposure. Multi-POV permitted; the reveal re-routes both testimonies.
