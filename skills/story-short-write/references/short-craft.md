# Short-Story Writing: The General Base

> The shared parent class for English short-form commercial fiction (4000-12000 words) across Wattpad / Tapas / Inkitt / Radish / Galatea / Dreame / GoodNovel — not traditional literary fiction.
> Genre style packs (`genre-styles/`) layer their flavor on top of this base; this file governs what all genres share.
> **Master principle: emotion runs hot, not tepid. The short story is an emotion product — it keeps readers through direct resonance, not restraint and white space.**

---

## 1. POV and voice: the experiencing narrator, dual-track switching

- **Default first person for emotional genres** (Wattpad YA romance, Radish contemporary, Galatea drama) — the reader climbs inside the protagonist's body and head. **Close third for thriller/mystery/suspense** (Inkitt, Galatea multi-POV, Kindle shorts). The POV decision is made per platform + genre in Phase 2 and recorded in `setting.md`; switching mid-draft breaks immersion.
- **Voice is dual-track, switched by the "heart-dead" toggle** (for hurt/comfort genres) or the "armor-slip" toggle (for romance):

| Stage | Voice | How |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Hurt phase | Direct venting | Name the pain, the tears that won't behave, the dead weight in the chest; runs of interiority and chain questions |
| Counterattack / reversal phase | Cold judgment | Detached, ironic, looking down; short, bloodless, "I actually just find you funny." |

- **The heart-dead freeze line is the switch hub**: after the truth is broken open, one standalone heart-dead declaration ("That was the moment I stopped loving him. I mean that. Stopped.") flips the plot from patient grief to cold rupture; all later behavior follows from the flip.

---

## 2. Externalizing emotion: name the emotion, then land it on a scene-specific action (replaces the mechanical replacement table)

The most central short-form technique — and the one that clashes hardest with long-form aesthetics.

In real commercial short fiction, emotion words (heart like dead weight, sick to the stomach, tears not behaving) are written outright, and densely — that's the selling point, not AI-flavor. The key is the next sentence: **the emotion word must land on a concrete action or object specific to this scene, right now — the emotion word never floats alone.**

Write like real published shorts (rhythm to imitate):

- She held her smile until her jaw ached. The wedding cake was in the trunk, the groom was inside, and the text on his phone said "she's asleep, bring the wine."
- My heart was a stone in my chest. I turned the ring over in my palm — the one he'd given me at the lake, the summer we were nineteen — and dropped it in the storm drain.
- The anger came up like bile. I gripped the edge of the counter until my knuckles went white, then I picked up the phone and called the lawyer.

Common thread: the emotion word lands on **the specific thing in hand, in view, right now** — the cold soup, the broken jade, the corner of the sleeve, the lips bitten to blood. Not the universal physiological fillers any story can wear ("nails digging into the palm," "heart dropping," "nose stinging" alone read hollow).

The emotion can also land on a task blocker: the protagonist has something that must get done this moment and gets stuck at this step. In shorts the task blocker must be short, sharp, and emotion-glued: the blocked life-saving money, the blocked evidence, the blocked exit, the blocked call for help, the blocked counterattack. Its purpose is not to describe a procedure or fake naturalness — it's to make the reader immediately feel "more humiliated / more desperate / more obliged to fight back."

**Two things to delete:**
1. Emotion-summary sentences with nothing concrete after them ("a sliver of sadness surfaced," "a deep despair settled over her," "it was as if the world had lost its color").
2. The same emotion written twice — once for the feeling, once explaining how much it hurt. Name it once, land it once, move on.

## 3. Narrative stance: the present narrator (distinct from long-form deep limited)

The short-form narrator is **highly present**: she may step out of the moment to judge, foreshadow, sentence, or preview the payoff for the reader. This is the satisfaction engine — not AI-flavor.

- Keep: first-person **subjective verdicts** ("So that's what our love was. A punchline."), **revenge previews** ("They don't know I've already sent the file."), **payoff forward-tells** ("I didn't know it yet, but he was already tearing the city apart looking for me." / "Later, he'd say he was sorry. Later was too late.").
- Cut: true neutral omniscient camera voice — author exposition with no relation to the character's emotion.

Test: does this sentence, stepping out of the moment, carry the protagonist's biased judgment / pull the read forward? Keep it. Is it neutral, emotionless author explanation? Cut it.

## 4. Section structure: micro-chapter system, one blast per section

Two rhythms by platform:

| Platform | Section marker | Paragraphs | Words per section |
|----------|----------------|------------|-------------------|
| Tapas / Wattpad one-shots / Radish one-shots | `###` + number (`###1.`) | Short, heavy line breaks | 400-800 |
| Wattpad serials / Inkitt / Galatea / Dreame / GoodNovel | `###Part N` / `###Chapter N` or `###1.` | Short base, multi-sentence retrospectives interwoven with dialogue | 800-1500 (romance 800-2000) |
| Serialized episodes (Radish/Galatea/Inkitt/Wattpad) | episode = 3-6 sections | per the platform's paragraph base | 1500-3000 per episode |

- Each section: **one betrayal escalation / one attitude flip / one harsher line / one new piece of reader information**.
- Section ends never settle the emotion; always leave a hook.
- **Dense/sparse**: betrayal / comeuppance / reversal / rupture beats are written dense, slow-motion, beat by beat; transitions / travel / information delivery pass in one line, giving the word budget to the emotion nodes.
- A task blocker must serve "one betrayal escalation / one attitude flip / one new information"; it cannot become standalone life-procedure; if it produces no emotion or evidence gain, don't write it.

## 5. Hooks: very high density, laddered escalation

- The blurb (50-220 words) packs 3-5 events/reversals; the body delivers nearly one new reveal or attitude flip per section; Tapas-density formats turn every 3-5 sentences.
- **Laddered escalation**: moral trampling → financial → life-and-death, each step larger. One page may stack multiple mines (the fraud + the public kiss + the child's medication) — never dwell on a single point.
- **Payoffs push forward every chapter**: every section must advance a counter-move / an action / a revealed card (the slap, the canceled wedding, the filed report, the public exposure, the identity flash) — don't park on an emotion loop; section ends carry the counterattack preview ("the lawyer has been called." "I have his password."), creating the read-on gap. The reader wants "next section, I want to see her win."
- **Public witness amplifies humiliation**: put betrayal / comeuppance where outsiders can see (the gala, the shareholder meeting, the group chat screenshot, the family dinner) — the crowd's jeering / contempt / silence magnifies both the shame and the vindication; a solo scene can't produce "public shame."
- Shared hook motifs (genre packs detail them): the rival/ex-triggering betrayal, the overheard/caught truth hitting all at once, the crisis-time plea answered with contempt, the social-post/evidence confirm, the numbers/years-built-then-zeroed, the identity-card under the humiliation, public shame + reversal countdown.

## 6. Dialogue: high ratio, three jobs of a line

- Ratio ~40-60% (comeuppance pieces can hit 60%; lyrical hurt pieces can sit at 30-40%). **Float by emotion-driven vs event-driven; no dead line.**
- Three line types: ① the abuser's moral trampling / PUA cruelty (the hook itself — the more self-righteous the better); ② the rival's "sweet and wounded" soft blade (sorry / I didn't mean to / it's all my fault); ③ the protagonist's cold judgment one-liners after the heart-dead flip.
- Power play runs on dialogue-length inversion: the abuser's early long monologues flip to the protagonist's one-line cold snaps. See `dialogue-mastery.md`.

---

## 7. Endings: payoff preview / judgment line / walked-away exit

Short-story closers are never literary elevation:

| Closer | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Payoff reversal | After she leaves, the abuser regrets madly / searches wildly; she is unmoved or cold ("You want me back? I want the check.") |
| Judgment/irony line as epitaph | "Late love is cheaper than grass." / "To them I was the hole in the embroidery. Fine. The hole held the needle." |
| The walked-away exit | Signed divorce papers, married someone else, left with the kids, the identity flash |
| The indifference close | "That's their story now. It isn't mine." — stronger than revenge |

- Cut: hollow philosophical elevation ("that's just life," "she finally understood").
- Keep: first-person revenge previews / judgment lines / heart-dead residue — these are the screenshot-and-share highlights.

## 8. Rhythm and punctuation

- Short paragraphs, frequent line breaks, mobile scroll: narration uses comma-linked sentences, but short-form whole sentences run short and many paragraphs are single-sentence — land the period around 15-25 words, don't drag commas into endless river sentences. Short sentences are occasional emotional heavy beats at peaks, not the whole-text default.
- Punctuation follows tone: question marks for interrogation; a few exclamation marks at burst peaks; hesitation / unfinished / interrupted done with short sentences, action beats, or line breaks.
- Triple-parallel is a device for piling suffering ("at the wedding, at the hospital door, at her father's funeral"): **functional escalation/enumeration stays; rhetorical filler with no information gets cut.**
- **Punctuation hard constraint**: this repo's body output never contains `...` / `——` / `—` / `--` (de-AI scripts and hooks enforce it). Pauses, sobs, unfinished speech, white space: short sentences, periods, commas, action beats, line breaks. If a genre pack's example keeps `...`, it's for illustration only — re-write per this rule in the body.

## 9. The fundamental difference from long form

| Dimension | Long-form rule | Short-form base |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| POV | Deep limited, no foreshadowing | Present narrator, active payoff previews |
| Emotion | Body action replaces emotion words | Emotion words written outright + landed on concrete reaction |
| Chapter end | No summary / elevation / preview | Judgment lines / payoff previews are selling points |
| Structure | Three-appearance objects / number ladders | Overhearing / hidden cards / boomerangs / count accumulation |
| Interiority | Monologue ≤ 2 paragraphs | Runs of venting monologue + chain questions are the main engine |
| Parallel | Cut to one | Functional suffering-piling stays |

## 10. Scene writing: weave the three dimensions

Each sub-event isn't one sentence — it has three dimensions, **woven into the same continuous passage, never written dimension by dimension**:

| Dimension | Content | Point |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| Occurrence | The thing happens | 1-2 sentences with one concrete detail |
| Perception | What the protagonist notices | At least 1 sensory detail, focused on one object or body part |
| Reaction | How the body answers | A specific body action (hand / eye / breath / posture), may carry one very short mental freeze |

Task blockers weave the same way: occurrence = the thing won't get done; perception = the protagonist sees/touches/hears the concrete blockage; reaction = colder, angrier, deader on the spot, or instantly switching to the next counter-move. Never write the procedure as a standalone explanation paragraph; never tack an action tail on scene ends to explain meaning.

Anti-pattern (forbidden): splitting the three dimensions into three sequential paragraphs — the same beat written three times.

- Correct (woven): I thought he was really apologizing, until my hand closed around the soup bowl — cold, the fat congealed on top, the way it is when something's been sitting for hours. My heart went colder than the bowl. (touching the soup, the congealed oil, the cold heart — all in one passage)
- Wrong (stacked): I touched the soup bowl. / The soup was cold, fat congealed on top. / My heart went cold. (the same beat in three sentences — the reader reads it three times)

**Picture-based paragraphing**: woven ≠ one paragraph to the end. Break at new action / new object / new information / new dialogue / viewpoint shift — not by dimension. One paragraph carries one camera shot or one continuous line of reasoning / atmosphere / emotion chain.

## 11. Dense/sparse allocation + action and quiet

**Dense/sparse**: not every beat gets the same length and detail (uniform effort is the root of AI-flavor).

| Beat type | Ink |
|-----------|-----|
| Payoff / comeuppance / reversal / emotion peak | Dense (detailed; perception-action-dialogue filled; slow-motion beat by beat) |
| Transition / travel / information delivery / time jump | Sparse (1-2 sentences; no three-dimension expansion) |
| Setup / daily / relationship warm-up | Medium (one or two immersive details made real, the rest skimmed) |

**Action and quiet alternate**: an action-impact section is followed by a quiet perception section. Emotion peaks (comeuppance / reversal) use "action"; the low points (heart-dead / residue) use "quiet." Never 2 consecutive all-action sections (violence fatigue), never 2 consecutive all-quiet (dragging).

**Subject rhythm**: name the protagonist at paragraph start / scene switch / multi-character scenes; flow with pronouns / elision inside one action chain; name again at key turns. Avoid consecutive sentences opening with the same protagonist name.

---

## 12. Opening event density

First 100 words ≥ 3 events. Delete all background introduction ("she was the kind of girl who…", "in the year 18XX…"); the first sentence is an event / dialogue / information bomb, not a description.

**The opening paragraph must drop a quantifiable, screenshot-able, life-grade / shame-grade stake** — the life-saving money, the lost child, the public-proof humiliation — within three seconds. No slow interior warm-up, no dwelling on a single point. The stake and its details need **non-replaceable distinctiveness** (a specific number, a specific object) — nothing swappable, nothing you could rename and resell as generic.

- High density (model): My husband locked me in the house so I wouldn't bother him at the concert with his secretary. The apartment caught fire. I called him, and he said, "Can't you be a little reasonable? It's two tickets." — three strikes in three lines: the lock, the fire, the cold refusal.
- Low density (avoid): spending the opening on "who she is, her family background, what the weather was like."
