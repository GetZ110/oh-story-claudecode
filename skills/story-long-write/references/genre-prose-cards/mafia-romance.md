# Mafia Romance Prose Card

## Prose directive
Mafia romance runs on transactional intimacy: everything is a deal, including the marriage. The world is built from obligations — blood, debt, territory, names — and the romance must live inside those obligations, not beside them. The prose should be economical and watchful: characters count exits, notice hands, read rooms. Violence is brief, professional, and always consequential. The heat comes from trust being extended inside a system where trust is the most dangerous thing to hold.

## Opening hooks
- Cut in at the exchange: a bride handed over to settle a debt, a witness needing protection, a daughter bargaining for her family.
- First scene establishes the rule of the house — who gives orders, who pays, what happens to words.
- The heroine enters with an agenda of her own; the marriage is a battlefield, not a cage.

## Conflict engine
Obligation + distrust + competing loyalties (family vs marriage, house vs heart). The romance runs on the question of whose side either of them takes when the guns come out.

## Payoff & emotion release
Payoffs come from public loyalty, the family being forced to respect her, a betrayal predicted and countered, and the moment the lead chooses her over the house. Release on actions: a seat at the head of the table, a name defended, a debt paid in her name.

## Dialogue & voice
The lead speaks in commands and silences; his dialogue is the shortest in the room. The heroine speaks the family's language fluently — she was raised inside it or learns it fast. Conversations are negotiations; even affection is phrased as a deal.

## Chapter-end hooks
Use a family council summons, an old enemy resurfaces, a loyalty test, a wire or a witness, the marriage contract's hidden clause, or a funeral that changes the hierarchy. The hook tightens the noose around both the plot and the marriage.

## Scene granularity
The texture lands on family dinners where seating is rank, ledgers, wedding contracts, safe houses, church attendance as alibi, names crossed off lists, and the gun he puts down before he touches her. Authenticity comes from the rules, not the brand of car.

## Prose landing points
Open on the exchange or the bargain; conflict lands on obligation and its loopholes; endings land on a public choice between the house and her — made in front of witnesses.

## Early/mid/late play
- Early: establish the bargain and the rules of the house; intimacy begins as leverage.
- Mid: alternate house politics with private trust; every secret shared is a card on the table.
- Late: force the public choice; the ending settles whether the marriage was a transaction that became real.

## Pacing density
One house-politics or loyalty beat per chapter. No chapter without a visible cost or a visible rule.

## This-chapter tradeoffs
Name the obligation and the loophole. Keep the heroine's agenda alive; she is never only the prize.

## Drift bans
No glamorizing violence as courtship; no heroine who exists to be protected; no family rules that conveniently vanish for the romance; no happy ending that costs nothing. The ledger stays open until the last page.

## Sample passages

> 1. The contract was read aloud at the family table, as tradition demanded. Two properties, a shipping lane, and the daughter of the Moretti family. Elena kept her face still through the list, counting the men at the table the way her father had taught her: seven, all armed, all smiling like the deal was already done. When the reading ended, the man she was being sold to said one word. "No." The table went silent. He looked at her. "Two properties, the lane, and she keeps her name." "That wasn't the agreement." "It is now."

> 2. He didn't apologize for the blood on his knuckles. He washed it off at the kitchen sink, slow, methodical, while she stood in the doorway holding the first-aid kit she'd bought that morning on the theory that a wife in this house should be prepared. "You knew I'd come home like this." "I knew you'd come home." He looked at her then, for a long moment, and something in his face loosened a fraction. "The kit stays," he said. "It's the first thing you've brought into this house that wasn't part of the deal."

> 3. The wire was in her coat, taped under the collar, and the FBI agent on the other end was counting on her fear. She walked into the warehouse with it, let them pat her down, let them find nothing — because she'd turned it off before she got out of the car and left it dead against her skin like a stone. She gave the meeting everything they wanted to hear. Then she drove home, unpinned the wire, and laid it on the kitchen counter in front of her husband. "Your turn," she said. "Tell me what you knew."
