# Cultivation (Xianxia) Prose Card

## Prose directive
Cultivation fiction is a world built from ranks, resources, and faces. The prose must always know the protagonist's cultivation level, what resource they are missing, why the enemy can press them, and what this action gains or costs. The genre voice favors spare, weight-bearing sentences for the mundane and slow, sensory expansion at the peaks — breakthroughs, sect trials, duels, and the moment a face is lost or saved in public. Face is currency; write it like money.

## Opening hooks
- Cut in at the humiliation or the gate: a dumped engagement, a family trial, a sect exam, a resource seized, a secret realm opening, a strong enemy approaching.
- First scene establishes the ladder and the protagonist's rung — and the debt or grudge that forces the climb.
- The protagonist's flaw (broken core, crippled meridians, a stolen technique) is the engine, not the decoration.

## Conflict engine
Realm suppression + resource scarcity + position on the ladder. Growth shows rung by rung; breakthroughs carry costs and witnesses. The sect, the clan, and heaven itself are all ranked.

## Payoff & emotion release
Release comes from over-tier wins, resources secured, realm breakthroughs, identity flips, and new-map qualifications. Every payoff leaves a higher pressure visible. The upgrade must be felt in the body — new senses, new weight in the room, new fear in elders' eyes.

## Dialogue & voice
Clan heads, sect elders, rivals, and mentors speak by rank: formal address, measured threats, silence that costs more than words. The protagonist's lines are short and hard but must state a goal, a judgment, or a bottom line. No uniform hot-blooded shouting.

## Chapter-end hooks
Use the next trial, the secret-realm gate, a rival's arrival, a resource exposure, an elder's changed attitude, or a new qualification. The end always raises the next rung's pressure.

## Scene granularity
The texture lands on proving grounds, assessment stones, spirit pills, technique fragments, beast corpses, sect tokens, resource ledgers, wounds, and watching disciples. Every setting noun hangs on a concrete gain or danger.

## Prose landing points
Open on the trial, the test, the stolen resource, or the realm gate; conflict lands on realm suppression and resource gaps; endings land on a new trial, dungeon qualification, a rival arriving, or an elder changing their tune.

## Early/mid/late play
- Early: press the despised opening with tests, seized resources, family humiliation, or qualifications; show the upgrade gap clearly.
- Mid: settle around realms, pills, rankings, arenas, and secret-realm gains; comeuppance ties to resources landing.
- Late: drag grudges, sect interests, and higher powers into one settlement; end on a stronger rival, realm depths, or an elder's changed stance.

## Pacing density
Early rhythm runs the five-step loop: pressured, trial, evidence/resources gained, small turnaround, higher pressure. Between breakthroughs and over-tier fights there must be preparation; no cost-free consecutive upgrades.

## This-chapter tradeoffs
Pick one spine per chapter — training, resources, or combat — and let the others assist. Don't stack realms, maps, techniques, bloodlines, and factions at once; the prose must not become a setting table.

## Drift bans
No empty hot-blooded shouting; no level-table dumping; no cost-free breakthroughs; no combat without a resource chain and goal chain underneath; no face-loss scenes without witnesses.

## Sample passages

> 1. The assessment stone was cold against Lin Chen's palm, and cold was all it gave. The elder's voice carried to the last row of the hall: "Qi Refinement, ninth grade. No marrow, no bloodline, no master's seal." The hall laughed the way halls laugh — in layers, the front row polite, the back row loud. Lin Chen bowed, and on the way down his sleeve caught the stone, once, the way his grandmother had taught him. The stone flickered. Nobody saw it. The elder's eyes did, and the elder said nothing, and Lin Chen walked out carrying a debt the hall didn't know it had accrued.

> 2. The breakthrough cost him the winter's spirit stones, a pill his master had guarded for a year, and the feeling in his left hand for six hours. When it came back, it came back with a pulse he could hear: his own blood moving like a tide, and beneath it, the faint brass note of the sect's grand formation. He could hear the mountain. He opened his eyes; his master stood over him with the ledger open. "Well," the old man said. "Now you can pay rent."

> 3. The rival's champion had won twelve duels that season, and the crowd chanted his name like a war drum. Yan Xi stepped onto the stone with one hand behind her back. The ceremony elder frowned. "You'll fight as you are?" "As I am," she said. The champion swung. The roar had not yet peaked when the stone beneath his feet turned to water — her technique, bought with a favor, drilled until the motion was a breath. He went down on his own momentum, and she set her foot on the water and waited for the count. Twelve duels, the hall would learn, was not the same as one.

## Corpus notes (initial calibration)

- Dialogue share: ~20-30%; challenge, negotiation, and court scenes run higher
- Paragraph length: 2-3 sentences base; breakthrough/duel set-pieces 4-6, kept rare
- Pacing: one visible gain or loss per chapter; breakthrough after preparation, never back-to-back
